# 2601.22101 — ECO: Quantized Training without Full-Precision Master Weights (ECO)

## 2. Summary

Low-precision training still keeps a full-precision copy of the weights, called the master weights (usually
FP32, 4 bytes per parameter). Updates are accumulated there because many steps are smaller than the gap
between low-precision grid points; applied directly to quantized weights, those updates vanish. ECO
(Error-Compensating Optimizer) removes the master copy. After each optimizer step it quantizes the new weights
(for example to FP8), computes the rounding error, and adds that error into the optimizer's momentum buffer.
That forms an error-feedback loop with no extra memory. Under standard assumptions and a decaying learning
rate (LR), ECO provably converges to a constant-radius neighbourhood of the optimum. Removing master weights
naively gives an error that scales like 1/LR, which explodes as the LR decays. Across 30M–800M dense models,
Gemma 3 1B, a 2.1B sparse MoE, and INT4 fine-tuning of DeepSeek-MoE-16B, ECO with stochastic rounding (SR)
nearly matches master-weight baselines.

## 3. Method

- **Update** (Alg. 1–2, Sec. 3.2), with weights stored only in quantized form `θ̂`:
  1. Optimizer step on `θ̂`: `θ̃ = θ̂ + u`.
  2. Quantize: `θ̂' = q(θ̃)`.
  3. Rounding error: `e = θ̃ − θ̂'`.
  4. Inject into momentum: `m ← m + (1/η)(1 − 1/β)·e`.
  For Adam (Alg. 3), the scalar LR η is replaced by Adam's element-wise effective step
  `η / ((1−β1^t)(√(v/(1−β2^t)) + ε))`.
- **Why the rule is memory-free** (Sec. 3.2, App. A): the exact rule, which reproduces master-weight momentum
  SGD (SGDM) step for step, needs both `e_t` and `e_{t+1}`, so it has to store the previous error. ECO
  assumes `e_t ≈ e_{t+1}` and drops that buffer. Consecutive errors are similar because the update is dominated
  by slowly changing momentum. On the 30M model the relative norm `‖e_{t+1}‖/‖e_t‖` stays close to 1 and the
  cosine similarity stays high (Fig. 2).
- **Theory** (Sec. 3.3–3.4, App. B–C), for the SGDM variant with unbiased (SR) quantization: Theorem 3.8 gives
  `min E‖∇f‖² ≤ 4(f(θ0) − f*)/(ηT) + σ²_quant`, with a noise floor tending to `4L²σ²/(1−β²)` as η → 0. On a
  quadratic, the limits are: master weights `L²σ²`; ECO `L²σ²/(1−β²)`; naive removal `∝ 1/η`, which
  diverges. With round-to-nearest (RTN), the floor scales like `(1−β)^-2`, which is worse (Theorem 3.10).
- **Setup:**
  - Quantization is FP8 E4M3 with row-wise scales, applied only to the linear layers inside transformer blocks.
    Embeddings and the output head are not quantized. Activations use RTN; weights use RTN or SR (Sec. 4.1).
  - Optimizer: AdamW with β = (0.9, 0.98) and ε = 1e-9 (Sec. 4.2).
  - ECO needs no extra hyperparameter tuning, and its injection is an element-wise operation with negligible
    runtime cost (Sec. 1, 4.2).

## 4. Key reported results

- **Scaling study** (Table 1): 30M–800M parameters, C4 data, 100·N tokens (5× Chinchilla), batch 512,
  sequence length 512. Validation loss at 800M: BF16 with FP32 master weights 2.5306; FP8 with master weights
  (RTN) 2.5343; ECO + SR 2.5399; ECO + RTN 2.6046; naive removal + SR 2.9471. Naive removal + RTN diverges at
  every size.
- **Gemma 3 1B**, 40B C4 tokens (Fig. 3 left; labels read from the bars, pairing inferred; the figure says
  "Gemma 2 1B"):
  - Base (presumably BF16 with master weights) 2.7554; FP8 with master weights 2.7615 (RTN) / 2.7579 (SR);
    ECO + SR 2.7671; naive + SR 3.1103.
- **SMoE 2.1B** (sparse mixture of experts): 32 experts with 4 active, 24 layers, hidden size 576, trained on
  LM1B tokens equal to 100× active parameters; only the expert linear layers are quantized. Results (Fig. 3
  left):
  - Base 2.8829; FP8 with master weights 2.9144 (RTN) / 2.9177 (SR); ECO + SR 2.9232; naive + SR 3.0997.
- **Memory** (Sec. 4.4): for the SMoE with activation checkpointing and no gradient accumulation, peak memory is
  dominated by master weights and Adam states. Replacing the FP32 master with FP8 weights cuts it from 12 to 9
  bytes per parameter (about 25%). Fig. 1 shows the resulting static-memory vs. loss Pareto shift.
- **DeepSeek-MoE-16B fine-tuning** (Sec. 4.5, Table 2): INT4 weight-only quantization-aware training (QAT),
  tensor-wise.
  - Naive master-weight removal diverges with both RTN and SR (Fig. 3 right).
  - ECO matches the master-weight runs. Example, MMLU: 37.87 (master weights + RTN), 38.63 (ECO + RTN),
    38.41 (ECO + SR).

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **Memory per card, the binding limit.** A volunteer can drop the FP32 master copy and keep FP8 weights plus
  optimizer state. The paper's own accounting gives 9 bytes per parameter with FP32 Adam states, against 12
  with an FP32 master. Adding 8-bit Adam states would give about 3 bytes per parameter (our arithmetic; ECO is
  untested with quantized momentum, which must also hold the injected error). For static state on a 24 GB
  card that means about 2.7B parameters at 9 B/param, against about 8B at 3 B/param, before activations. If it
  holds up, the 4B stage, and perhaps 9B on 24 GB cards, could stay pure data-parallel instead of needing
  pipeline stages over the wide-area network. Data-parallel is the simplest layout to score, as in Covenant's
  LossScore.
- **Bytes per round.** If the network's canonical model is FP8, a join or resync download is 1 byte per
  parameter instead of 2 (BF16): 9 GB for 9B weights, about 12 minutes at 100 Mbit/s (our arithmetic). A
  possible new design, untested: aggregators also keep the global model in FP8 and fold each outer step's
  rounding error into the outer momentum of DiLoCo (local steps, then an outer optimizer step on averaged
  deltas). That removes the FP32 global master and halves weight broadcast against BF16.
- **Hardware.** The ECO step is element-wise and runs on any device. FP8 compute needs FP8 tensor cores
  (RTX 4090/4060 Ti/5090), but storing FP8 and computing in BF16 also works on an RTX 3090 or Apple silicon by
  dequantizing (general fact, not from the paper). ECO's guarantees rely on SR (Limitations), so SR must be
  implemented where the hardware lacks it.
- **Muon caution.** ECO's rule is derived for SGDM and Adam, where momentum feeds the update linearly. Muon
  orthogonalizes momentum, so an injected error term would pass through a nonlinear map. ECO with Muon
  (2607.20548) is an open question, not a free combination.
- **Trust and verification.** ECO moves hidden state into momentum, and Crowd's audit already treats error
  feedback as non-reproducible (DEEPDIVE.md). To replay a worker, an auditor needs:
  - the worker's starting momentum, through a per-round state commitment (hash), or inner state reset each
    round;
  - SR random bits drawn from a public, seeded counter-based generator.
  A cheater can also hide bias in the "rounding error". The noise floor in Theorem 3.8 gives a scale for what
  honest rounding noise looks like.
- **Round-1 links.**
  - SparseLoCo/Covenant-72B (2603.08163) already relies on error feedback (EF), and it keeps a separate EF
    buffer per peer that it offloads during compute. ECO shows such residuals can sometimes live in an existing
    buffer for free. Whether SparseLoCo's EF and the inner momentum could share one buffer is open.
  - In Decoupled DiLoCo (2604.21428), the syncer could keep FP32 while learners run FP8-only with ECO.
  - SOLO (2609.35440) cuts activation memory; ECO cuts parameter memory. The two are complementary.
  - Quantized Evolution Strategies (2602.03120, cited in round-1 texts) applies the same error-feedback idea
    to zeroth-order fine-tuning.

## 6. Caveats

- Pretraining scale is small: at most 2.1B total parameters (SMoE, LM1B data) and Gemma 3 1B at 40B tokens,
  with sequence length 512 throughout. Nothing is tested at the 4B–27B or trillion-token scale.
- FP8 only for pretraining (INT4 only for fine-tuning); no FP4 pretraining. Only linear layers in transformer
  blocks are quantized.
- The `e_t ≈ e_{t+1}` heuristic is checked only on the 30M model (Fig. 2). Gains depend on SR; the RTN variant
  is clearly worse (Table 1).
- No data-parallel, local-update, low-bandwidth, or heterogeneous-GPU experiments are described, and there is no
  interaction with optimizer-state quantization or Muon.
- The Gemma and SMoE numbers are read from bar labels in Fig. 3, with the pairing of bars to methods inferred.
