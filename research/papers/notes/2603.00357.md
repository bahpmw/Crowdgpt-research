# 2603.00357 — SPARe: Stacked Parallelism with Adaptive Reordering for Fault-Tolerant LLM Pretraining Systems with 100k+ GPUs (SPARe)

- **Authors:** Jin Lee (UC Santa Barbara; Argonne), Zhonghao Chen, Xuhang He, Xiaoyi Lu (University of Florida),

- **Date:** arXiv v1, 27 Feb 2026; the text read is v3, 27 May 2026 (cs.DC). **Venue:** ICML 2026.

## 2. Summary

At 100k+ GPUs, failures arrive faster than the system can restart. The mean time between failures (MTBF) falls as
1/#GPUs, while a global restart (mostly re-initialising collective communication) grows roughly linearly with #GPUs.
The authors call this the "restart-dominant regime" and project a failure every 30 min at 96k GPUs and every 5 min at
600k (§1). Checkpointing cuts lost work but not restart downtime, and full replication masks failures at r× compute.

SPARe instead pre-places r copies of each *data shard* across the data-parallel (DP) groups. A cyclic Golomb-ruler
layout ensures any two shards share at most one host group. The gradient all-reduce fires as soon as every shard type
has been computed by some survivor. After a failure, SPARe:
- shrinks the communicators;
- reorders the work with a matching algorithm;
- recomputes the lost shards in the same step.

It masks about as many failures as r-way replication for about 2–2.8× compute. In a discrete-event simulation of a
600k-H100 cluster, it cuts time-to-train by 40–50% compared with replication plus checkpointing.

## 3. Method

- **Setup (§2.1).** N DP groups (model replicas), each a model-parallel group of M GPUs. One node failure takes out its
  whole group, and a plain DP step needs all N partial gradients.
- **Placement (App. B.1).** Shard type i lives on the groups H_i = {(i − g) mod N : g ∈ G}, where G is an optimal
  Golomb ruler of length r (all pairwise differences distinct mod N; requires N ≥ 2·g_{r−1} − 1). Two types share at
  most one host (Lemma B.2), so "wipe-outs" (a type losing every host) are nearly independent.
- **Execution (Alg. 1).** Each group keeps an ordered stack of its r types. S_A is the number of stacks computed per
  step; it starts at 1, which is plain DP with no extra compute. Failures are detected when an all-reduce fails.
- **Reordering controller RECTLR (Alg. 2, App. D).**
  1. A Hopcroft–Karp bipartite matching checks whether the current S_A still covers all N types.
  2. If not, it finds the smallest S_A that covers all types when stacks may be freely reordered. If none ≤ r exists,
     a wipe-out has happened and the system restarts globally.
  3. Min-cost max-flow reorders the stacks with the fewest moves.
  The lost shards are then recomputed ("patch compute"), the communicators shrink, and the all-reduce runs. The gradient
  equals the failure-free one, because only *who* supplies each shard changes (§3.1).
- **Theory (§4).**
  - Thm 4.1: failures masked before the first wipe-out, µ(N,r) ≈ Γ(1/r)/r · N^(1−1/r). This is the same as replication.
  - Thm 4.2: overhead S(N,r) = mean of c(k) + ρ_k, where c(k) = ⌈N/(N−k)⌉ with k groups lost and ρ_k is the
    patch-compute probability. c(k) = 2 for 1 ≤ k ≤ N/2 and 3 beyond.
  - Thm 4.3: with checkpointing at the availability-optimal period (Eqs. 1–2), the best redundancy is
    r* ≈ ⌊log2 N + 0.833⌋. The theory assumes exponential failures; the simulations use Weibull.
- **Simulation (§5.1, Table 1).** FedDES on SimGrid, modelling 600k H100s:
  - failures: MTBF 300 s, Weibull shape k=0.78; global restart 3,600 s;
  - workload: 10T-parameter model (20 TB), N ∈ {200, 600, 1000} groups, 256M tokens and 64 s of compute per stack;
  - communication: all-reduce 2/6/10 s; shrink and controller 0.1 s each;
  - run: checkpoint save 60 s, 10,000 steps, ×N(1, 0.05²) jitter on all events.
  Baselines: replication + checkpointing (Rep+CKPT) and checkpointing only (CKPT-only).

## 4. Key reported results

- **Failure-rate inputs (§1).** Llama 3 reports about 4 interruptions per 1,000 eight-GPU servers per day, a failure
  every ~3 h at 16k H100s. Scaling with MTBF ∝ 1/#GPU (Kokolis et al. 2025, valid above 32 GPUs) gives about 30 min at
  96k and 5 min at 600k GPUs. A 600k cluster "may spend ≥60-min on global restart after every failure".
- **Masking (§4.1, Fig. 4, Table 5).** N=600 with r=20 masks about 426 failures on average (formula 424.2, Monte
  Carlo 426.4; we checked the formula value by hand). That raises effective MTBF up to ~426× for about 2.8× compute,
  against 20× for replication.
- **Formula accuracy (App. C, 1,000 trials).** Mean absolute percentage error is 1.13% for µ and 0.60% for the
  all-reduce stack, with worst-case relative error ≤5.06%. Simulated overhead is within ≤4% of Eq. 5 (Fig. 8).
- **Time-to-train (Table 2; best r per scheme; 3 trials; T0 = failure-free time).**

  | N | Rep+CKPT time/T0 | Rep+CKPT availability | SPARe+CKPT time/T0 | SPARe r* | SPARe availability | Gain |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 200 | 6.07 | 61.74% | 2.92 | 9 | 87.00% | 51.9% |
  | 600 | 4.27 | 79.89% | 2.49 | 8 | 93.90% | 41.7% |
  | 1000 | 3.88 | 84.41% | 2.34 | 9 | 96.54% | 39.6% |

- **Baselines (§5.2).** CKPT-only "did not proceed more than a few steps"; Rep+CKPT did best at r=3.
- **Weak spot (§5.2).** At r=2 SPARe loses to replication, because Weibull k<1 front-loads failures. The predicted r*
  was 8/10/10 against simulated optima of 9/8/9.
- **Controller cost (App. D).** Under 100 ms for N of 10²–10³; the simulation charges 0.1 s per failure.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **A volunteer fleet is this regime at its extreme.** Departures, not hardware, set the MTBF. Illustrative arithmetic
  (ours, unverified): 1M volunteers with ~3-hour sessions means about 90 departures per second. No step can wait for
  "all", and nothing can restart globally. SPARe's invariant, "aggregate once every work unit is covered", belongs
  alongside:
  - Decoupled DiLoCo's quorum K (2604.21428);
  - Covenant-72B's fixed window with spare peers, 24.4 active vs 16.9 contributing (2603.08163);
  - Cross-Facility's leases (2610.03457).
- **Redundant pre-placement for orchestrators.** Give each work unit (a data block or an inner phase) r hosts in a
  Golomb-ruler-like layout, so one departure never removes two copies of the same pair of units. When a volunteer
  vanishes, a pre-assigned host already holds the data. In Crowd a wipe-out only means re-leasing the unit, as in
  DARL, not a global restart. Our best r is therefore probably well below r* ≈ log2 N, which assumes 1-hour restarts
  (inference).
- **The 2× overhead is a granularity effect.** c(k) = ⌈N/(N−k)⌉ jumps to 2 after the first loss because a whole shard is
  the smallest unit. With many small units per volunteer the overhead should approach N/(N−k) (inference, untested).
- **Trust synergy.** SPARe computes some shards twice and throws the duplicates away. Crowd could cross-check them to
  catch silent corruption (LLM-PRISM 2604.10390) or cheating (bucket B6). This needs deterministic or tolerance-based
  comparison (inference).
- **Exact trajectory.** SPARe keeps the failure-free gradient. DiLoCo-style elastic merging (Cross-Facility,
  Decoupled DiLoCo) changes the effective batch each round. A fixed global batch helps reproducibility and replay audits
  (inference).
- **Planning tool.** µ(N,r) and S(N,r) are closed-form and checked against Monte Carlo. Once volunteer churn is
  measured, they can size redundancy before anything is built (within the caveats below).

## 6. Caveats

- Simulation only (SimGrid/FedDES), with no real implementation or measurement at any scale. The authors say
  100k–600k GPU experiments are beyond academic access (App. E).
- Datacenter assumptions:
  - synchronous DP with homogeneous groups;
  - fail-stop failures, detected at all-reduce;
  - a fixed 1-hour restart.
- Not modelled: stragglers, hardware heterogeneity, SDC, malicious workers.
- The formulas assume independent failures. Correlated failures (same rack, ISP or region) need placement across
  failure domains (§4.1).
- Even the best scheme takes 2.3–2.9× the failure-free time in their harsh setting (Table 2).
- 2–3× compute is a big tax for volunteers if used naively. The payoff depends on wipe-out cost, which is small when
  work is reassigned by lease.
- All volunteer-churn extrapolations here are ours and unverified.
