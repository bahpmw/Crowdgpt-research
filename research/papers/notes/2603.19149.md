# 2603.19149 — Optimal Splitting of Language Models from Mixtures to Specialized Domains (Optimal Split)

## 1. Citation

- **Title:** Optimal Splitting of Language Models from Mixtures to Specialized Domains
- **Authors:** Skyler Seto, Pierre Ablin, Anastasiia Filippova, Jiayuan Ye, Louis Bethune, Angelos Katharopoulos,
  David Grangier (Apple; Jiayuan Ye also National University of Singapore)
- **Date:** arXiv v1 19 Mar 2026 (checked on the abs page; only version).
- **Venue:** ICML 2026 (PMLR vol. 306) and the ICLR 2026 DATA-FM workshop. Found by web search; the arXiv PDF does
  not state a venue.
- **Code:** none mentioned. Training used Megatron-LM; evaluation used lm-evaluation-harness.

## 2. Summary

"Split model training" pretrains one seed model on the whole corpus for D tokens. It then makes K copies and
continues pretraining (CPT) each copy only on its own semantic cluster for D′ tokens. The copies never
communicate. At inference, a nearest-centroid router reads a short prefix and sends the whole document to one
specialist. The paper asks how to divide a fixed budget D_T = D + K·D′ between the shared and split phases. It fits a
scaling law L(N, D, D_k) and solves for the optimal split point t_s. Larger models need more shared pretraining
before splitting pays off. At the predicted split, 16 specialists of 1.3B or 2.7B beat a single model of the same size
trained on all the data by about 1.5–2 points of average zero-shot accuracy, at equal training compute. The 2.7B
specialists also beat a 6.7B dense model, which has about 2.5× their per-token inference cost.

## 3. Method

1. **Cluster (App. E.1):** cut DCLM (DataComp-LM, a filtered web corpus) into non-overlapping 2,048-token windows,
   embed them with sentence-BERT MiniLM-L6-v2, and run balanced k-means with K clusters (16 by default; 4–64 tested).
   The centroids are the router.
2. **Shared phase (Eq. 3.1):** train one model on the full mixture for D tokens.
3. **Split phase (Eq. 3.3):** make K copies and train each only on cluster k for D′ tokens. Optimizer states are
   reset at the split, and training continues at the base model's constant learning rate (App. A). Total cost is
   D + K·D′ tokens.
4. **Inference (Eq. 3.4):** route by the nearest centroid of a prefix: the first 32 tokens for language modeling, or the
   question/context field for QA (Table 3). Top-1 retrieval accuracy from a 32-token prefix is 0.8775 (Table 5).
5. **Scaling law (Eq. 4.2–4.3):** L = E0 + EΔ(N, D_k) + A·(D_k^α1 + c·D^α2)^−1 + B·N^−κ, where EΔ is a
   double-sigmoid bias term. The law is fit with a Huber loss and basin-hopping (App. G.1). Splitting starts to pay at
   D_split, where K·∂L/∂D = ∂L/∂D′ (Eq. 5.2). The optimal t_s solves min L subject to D + K·D′ = D_T (Eq. 5.1).
6. **Models (App. A):** GPT-style models of 100M–2.7B parameters, with 125M and 6.7B as extra baselines. 1M-token
   batches, context 1,024, GPT-2 tokenizer (50K BPE), AdamW (0.9, 0.999), constant learning rate after warmup.

## 4. Key reported results

- **The law fits and extrapolates (Table 1):** fit on models ≤1.3B and tested on 2.7B, mean absolute error (MAE) is
  0.012 and R² 0.934. Holding out the larger D and D′ values gives MAE 0.021–0.023 and R² 0.979–0.982. Per-domain
  fits average test r = 0.9987 (Table 9). The law beats the Liew & Kato (2025) form (Table 7). The optimal
  pretraining fraction extrapolates from ≤1.3B fits to 2.7B (App. G.2, Fig. 12).
- **When to split (Fig. 4, §5.3):** D_split grows with model size. The optimal pretraining *fraction* falls as the
  budget grows, while the optimal pretraining *token count* rises. Splitting greedily, as soon as it starts to pay, is
  not optimal.
- **1.3B at 120/240/360B total tokens (Figs. 5–6):** splitting with no shared pretraining is much worse.
  Splitting at about 40B tokens roughly ties full pretraining, and accuracy peaks near the predicted t_s.
  Exception: at 125M trained to about 100× Chinchilla, splitting from step 0 beats full pretraining (App. J, §6.1).
- **Domain perplexity (Table 4):** at a 720B-token budget with t_s = 340B (47% shared; App. B), 1.3B split models
  beat the single model on all five Pile domains, by 9.33% on average (ArXiv 11.01→9.92, FreeLaw 5.76→4.85, GitHub
  3.74→3.42, PubMed 7.90→7.25, DM Math 8.14→7.81).
- **Zero-shot QA at the compute of 1× Chinchilla (about 20 tokens per parameter) for 6.7B, about 140B tokens
  (§6.3.2, Table 10):** t_s = 340B tokens (1.3B) and 220B (2.7B). Eight-task average accuracy (our arithmetic from
  Table 10): 1.3B single 62.3 vs split 64.3; 2.7B single 64.2 vs split 65.6; 6.7B dense 65.1. Example:
  ARC-Challenge 40.70→45.82 at 2.7B.
- **Fewer specialists at inference (Table 2):** using only 2 of the 16 specialists per task costs about 0.6–1.1
  points (task subset) and 1.9–3.1 points (MMLU subset).
- **Number of clusters (Figs. 8, 17):** K = 4 is best with little shared pretraining, and K = 16 is a good balance. For
  2.7B, K = 64 is best at every split point.
- **MoE (mixture-of-experts) variant (App. N, Table 11):** splitting a 3.8B-total/0.7B-active MoE into 16 copies
  improves perplexity in all 16 clusters (average 18.407→18.008, −2.2%).
- **Headline (Fig. 1b):** at large budgets, standard pretraining "requires up to 50× the amount of data" to match
  optimal split training. This appears to be computed from the fitted law rather than from a training run; the paper
  does not say (unverified).
- **Cost (App. B):** about 1,000 H100-hours per 100B tokens at 1.3B, and about 2,200 at 2.7B.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **Puts a number on the embarrassingly parallel share of pretraining.** At the reported optima, 53% of tokens (1.3B)
  and about 37% (2.7B: 220B of about 347B; our arithmetic) run as K fully independent jobs. The rest must be shared
  training: Covenant/SparseLoCo (2603.08163) or Decoupled DiLoCo (2604.21428).
- **A "trunk, then forest" shape for a million-GPU network.** The whole network trains a shared trunk. At t_s it forks
  into K sub-networks, each running its own DiLoCo on one cluster and never talking to the others. After the fork,
  global synchronization cost stops growing with network size, and K can grow with participation (64 tested). t_s can
  be set ahead of time from cheap small-model fits (App. G.2). Because it depends only on logged quantities (N, D, D_k,
  K), the fork time could be a deterministic, recorded epoch-pivot rule (our idea).
- **Specialists are consumer-card sized.** They are dense 1.3–2.7B models. By our rough estimate, they fit 16–24 GB
  cards with 8-bit optimizer state and activation checkpointing (bucket B5 topics; untested here), so pipeline crews
  are not needed at that size. Later they can be merged BTX-style into one MoE (the roadmap cites Branch-Train-MiX),
  or grown with expert upcycling (2604.19835). App. N shows splitting also helps MoE.
- **Trust: damage stays in one cluster.** A poisoned or lazy sub-network degrades only its own specialist. Each cluster
  has its own held-out set (10,000 documents per cluster, App. C.2.1), so each specialist gets its own scorecard and
  can be re-forked from the trunk checkpoint. Data that cannot be shared can stay in the split phase (Conclusion).
- **vs SOMA (2609.37899):** same cluster-branch-train idea, but with backprop at 1.3–2.7B and a predictive rule for
  *when* to branch. Branching almost immediately, as SOMA does, works only for heavily over-trained small models
  (App. J), which fits SOMA's 8M LSTMs. At billion scale, the shared phase is needed.

## 6. Caveats

- K specialists hold K× the total parameters (16× by default). Comparisons hold training compute and per-token
  inference compute equal, not memory or serving cost (§6.4.1).
- Each whole document goes to one specialist, chosen from a short prefix. Mixed-domain documents and misrouting
  (top-1 0.88 at 32 tokens) are not studied in depth.
- All training is centralized (Megatron-LM on H100s). There are no tests of slow links, heterogeneity, churn, or
  adversaries, and no decentralized split phase.
- Both arms use a constant learning rate with no cooldown, and optimizer state is reset at the split. Seeds and error
  bars are not reported (unverified).
- English DCLM only, context 1,024. The largest split model is 2.7B; 6.7B appears only as a baseline. The 1.3B
  long-budget runs repeat data (fewer than 2 passes; App. C.1). The "up to 50×" headline is a scaling-law projection.
- No code is released, and the router (MiniLM embeddings plus centroids) is a separate model.
