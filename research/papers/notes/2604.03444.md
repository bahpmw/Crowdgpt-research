## 2. Summary

This is a large-scale, closely matched test of a hybrid architecture against a transformer. Olmo Hybrid is Olmo 3 7B
with its sliding-window attention (SWA) layers, 75% of all layers, replaced by **Gated DeltaNet (GDN)** linear-recurrent
layers. The result is an interleaved pattern of 3 GDN layers to 1 full-attention layer. Model size is 7B, trained on
6T tokens on 512 GPUs. The hybrid reaches Olmo 3's Common Crawl loss with **35% fewer tokens** and its MMLU score
with **49% fewer tokens** (Fig. 1). It beats Olmo 3 in every base-evaluation domain after mid-training and scores
+14.1 points on RULER at 64k context. Controlled scaling laws (60M–1B, identical data and optimizer) show a lower
data coefficient B for the hybrid. That projects to about 1.3× fewer tokens at 1B and 1.7–1.9× at 7B–70B for the
same loss. The theory part shows that hybrids can express tasks that neither transformers nor GDN can alone
("state-based recall"). It then argues, in a simplified model, that extra expressivity improves data efficiency.

## 3. Method

- **GDN with negative eigenvalues (Def. 1):** S_t = S_{t−1} α_t (I − 2β_t k_t k_tᵀ) + v_t k_tᵀ and y_t = S_t q_t, with
  α, β ∈ (0, 1) and ‖k‖ = 1. Using "2β" in place of β allows negative eigenvalues, which lets GDN do state tracking.
- **Model (Sec 2.1–2.2, App. A.1):**
  - Olmo 3 7B layout with 30 heads (down from 32), GDN query/key dimension 96 and value dimension 192.
  - 7.0B parameters vs 6.8B for Olmo 3. Training throughput matched: 8.2K vs 8.0K tokens/s on 128 H100s (Table 9).
- **Training (App. A.1–A.2):**
  - Cosine LR to 10% of peak. Data: the Olmo 3 32B mix, not the Olmo 3 7B mix.
  - Hardware: 512 H100s, moved to 512 B200s about halfway through.
  - Mid-training: two 100B-token runs, merged.
  - Long-context extension: 100B tokens with YaRN or DroPE (drop RoPE). Ulysses context parallelism runs through
    both the attention and the GDN layers.
- **Scaling study (Sec 4.1, App. D):**
  - Sizes 60M–1B at 10–160 tokens per parameter. The WSD-S schedule gives 5 data budgets from one run per size.
  - Fit L(N, D) = E + A/N^α + B/D^β with a Huber loss and 1,000 bootstrap resamples.
  - Comparisons use a fixed-exponent fit (α = β = 0.22) for robustness.
- **Ablations (Sec 5.1):** GDN vs Mamba2; linear-to-attention ratios 1:1, 3:1 and 7:1; interleaved vs middle
  placement; output gate and eigenvalue sign.

## 4. Key reported results

- **Base evaluations after mid-training (Table 2; hybrid vs Olmo 3):**
  - Math 61.2 vs 59.8; Code 32.9 vs 32.1; STEM multiple choice (MC) 70.9 vs 67.3; non-STEM MC 81.3 vs 78.2;
    generative QA (GenQA) 73.6 vs 71.3.
  - Held-out sets: MMLU-Pro 43.1 vs 36.6; BBH 68.6 vs 65.9.
  - Before mid-training the hybrid was *worse* on Code (17.1 vs 19.6) and GenQA (66.8 vs 68.5).
- **Long context, RULER (Table 3):**
  - 64k: Olmo 3 (YaRN) 70.9; hybrid with YaRN 76.9; hybrid with DroPE 85.0.
  - 4k: hybrid slightly lower (92.2–92.8 vs 95.8).
- **Inference state per layer, fp16 (Table 1):** GDN 1.05 MiB; SWA with a 4k window 16 MiB; grouped-query attention
  (GQA) at 32k 128 MiB; multi-head attention (MHA) at 32k 512 MiB.
- **Scaling laws (Sec 4.1, Tables 18–20):**
  - Hybrid data coefficient B = 83.7 [80.2, 87.1] vs transformer 94.9 [88.7, 102.0], with non-overlapping CIs.
    Table 18 prints slightly different values.
  - The exponents do not differ significantly. In the fixed fit the transformer has the *lower* irreducible loss
    E (1.569 vs 1.597).
  - Projected token savings at target loss 2.474: **1B 1.32× [0.67, 4.02]; 3B 1.57× [1.11, 2.50];
    7B 1.68× [0.91, 3.47]; 13B 1.75×; 30B 1.82×; 70B 1.89×.** Pure GDN at 1B needs *more* data (0.82×).
  - The fits predict the final 7B losses within 0.28% (Table 4).
- **Ablations (Table 5, average bits per byte at 1B; lower is better):** transformer 0.682; pure GDN 0.677;
  **hybrid 3:1, 0.669**; 7:1, 0.675; 1:1, 0.674; Mamba2 3:1, 0.698. 7:1 is as good or better at 60M–190M.
  Interleaving beats putting attention in the middle.
- **Stability (Fig. 13):** the gradient-norm "spike score" stays low and flat for the hybrid but grows for Olmo 3
  (preliminary).
- **Post-training (Table 7):** mixed results. Think SFT gives MMLU +5.6 but AIME −2.4/−3.4; after DPO, BBH −12.0
  and AIME −10.2/−13.4.
- **Inference numerics (Sec 5.3):** correct scores required `--enforce-eager` or an FP32 GDN cache, because
  torch.compile's numerical differences compound through the recurrent state. In eager mode, throughput fell to
  0.5–0.9× of the dense model. At 32k, RL rollout cost was 44.2K GPU-hours for the hybrid (compiled) vs 73.8K for
  Olmo 3, and 58.9K for the hybrid in eager mode (Table 8).

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **More learning per token along the whole roadmap.** Projected savings grow with size: 1.32× at 1B, 1.57× at 3B
  (the only CI that excludes 1×), and about 1.7–1.8× in the 9B–27B range. Throughput per token is matched (Table 9).
  In a DiLoCo/SparseLoCo-style crowd (2603.08163, 2604.21428), fewer tokens to a target loss means
  proportionally fewer outer rounds and fewer bytes sent. This is a rare change that cuts compute *and*
  communication, and it is independent of the outer loop.
- **Memory on 8–24 GB cards.** Training memory does not change in kind. Inference and RL rollout state drops by
  15–485× per layer (Table 1), so volunteers can run long-context evaluation and rollouts that a dense model could
  not fit.
- **Composition.**
  - Dense here, but the hybrid-MoE designs Kimi Linear and Nemotron 3 Nano exist (Table 6), relevant to the
    ~27B MoE option.
  - With SOLO (2609.35440) and other pipeline or local-learning splits, stage-boundary activations are still
    B·T·d. Recurrent layers inside a module are untested there.
  - SOMA (2609.37899) used LSTM experts; GDN is the modern parallel-trainable recurrent choice.
- **Trust and replay audits (warning).** Small numerical differences change results through the recurrent state
  (Sec 5.3). Volunteers will run different GPUs, kernels and compile settings. Our design rules: keep GDN state in
  FP32, pin FLA kernel versions in the audit contract, and calibrate tolerance thresholds per device class. The
  paper does not measure training-time sensitivity (unverified).
- **Apple silicon gap (our assumption, unverified).** GDN training uses FLA's Triton kernels. We know of no Metal
  backend for these kernels, so Mac volunteers would need a separate chunked implementation with different
  numerics.

## 6. Caveats

- The 7B comparison is "largely comparable", not fully controlled. The hybrid used a different data mix (Olmo 3
  32B's), a cosine instead of a piecewise LR schedule, 2 fewer heads, and switched hardware mid-run.
- Savings projections come from fits at 60M–1B with wide CIs. The predicted loss gap at ≥1e21 FLOPs has a CI that
  includes 0 (Table 19). At 1B, our first stage, the projected gain is smallest.
- Post-training regressed on long reasoning, and inference is numerically fragile.
- The theory uses a simplified "quantization model" of scaling; the authors warn against reading its numbers
  literally.
- Nothing on consumer GPUs, low bandwidth, heterogeneity or low-communication training.
