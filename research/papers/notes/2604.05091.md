# 2604.05091 — MegaTrain: Full Precision Training of 100B+ Parameter Large Language Models on a Single GPU (MegaTrain)

## 1. Citation
- **Authors:** Zhengqing Yuan, Hanchi Sun, Lichao Sun, Yanfang Ye. University of Notre Dame and Lehigh University.
- **Date:** arXiv v1, 6 Apr 2026 (cs.CL). No venue stated.
- **Code:** https://github.com/DLYuanGod/MegaTrain (stated on p. 1; not checked).

## 2. Summary
Who can train a large model is decided by GPU memory, not compute. MegaTrain inverts the usual design. Host (CPU)
memory is the authoritative store for all parameters, gradients and optimizer states. The GPU is a stateless compute
engine: it streams in one layer's weights at a time, computes, streams that layer's gradients out, and drops the
weights. Double-buffered prefetch over three CUDA streams hides the transfers over PCIe (the CPU-GPU link).
"Stateless layer templates" replace PyTorch's persistent autograd graph, so device memory never holds much more than
one layer plus checkpointed activations. The result: 120B-parameter models on one H200 with 1.5 TB of RAM, and
14B-parameter models on a 24 GB RTX 3090. All evaluation is fine-tuning, not pretraining.

## 3. Method
- **One training step (Alg. 1, §3.1).**
  1. Streaming forward: stream in layer i, compute, checkpoint the activation every K layers, release the weights.
  2. Loss and LM-head backward.
  3. Block-wise backward: reload a checkpoint, recompute the block, stream weights in reverse, run each layer's
     backward, and offload its gradient to host memory.
  4. Adam update on the CPU (AVX-512 vector instructions), run asynchronously with GPU work.
- **Pipelined engine (§3.2).** Three streams: compute, host-to-device (H2D), and device-to-host (D2H).
  - Weight buffers alternate (ping-pong) so the next layer loads while the current one computes.
  - CUDA events (Weights-Ready, Backward-Done, Buffer-Free) order the work.
  - Overlap condition: each layer's transfer time (parameters / PCIe bandwidth) must hide under the previous
    layer's compute. Transfer volume per step does not depend on token count (§1), so large batches amortize it.
- **Memory layout (§3.3, App. A.1, A.3).**
  - Each layer's BF16 weights, BF16 gradients, and FP32 Adam moments m and v are packed into one contiguous block,
    aligned to 4 KB pages.
  - Two pinned (page-locked) staging buffers are sized to the largest layer, plus K = 12 pinned gradient slabs.
  - The master store lives in ordinary pageable memory.
- **Stateless execution (§3.4, App. A.4).** Templates hold the kernels but no weights; a Bind step maps streamed
  tensor views into them. No CUDA graph capture is used.
- **State floor (§2.1).** At least 12 bytes per parameter: BF16 weights (2), BF16 gradients (2), FP32 m and v (8).
  That is 840 GB for a 70B model.

## 4. Key reported results
- **GH200** (96 GB HBM3, 480 GB LPDDR5X, NVLink-C2C at 900 GB/s; §4.2, Fig. 1): 284 TFLOPS at 7B, 264 at 14B (1.84x
  ZeRO-3 Offload), and above 250 at 32B.
- **H200** (141 GB, PCIe, 1.5 TB host RAM): trains 72B and 120B (GPT-OSS-120B MoE). Throughput appears only in
  Fig. 1 (unverified).
- **Accuracy matches ZeRO-3 on MetaMathQA fine-tuning (Table 3).** 7B: 88.99% vs 88.93%. 14B: 92.52% vs 92.41%.
- **Ablation (Table 4).** Removing double buffering drops throughput from 266.3 to 182.9 TFLOPS (-31%).
- **Depth scaling (Table 5, Fig. 5).** Device allocation is fixed at 3.83 GB. Going from 28 to 180 layers, throughput
  drops from 284 to 227 TFLOPS. Host RAM grows from 115 GB (42 layers) to 418 GB (180 layers, 43B parameters).
  ZeRO-3 and FSDP run out of memory beyond 84 layers.
- **A100 PCIe (Fig. 7).**
  - 7B: 128 TFLOPS, 2.42x ColossalAI-Gemini and 3.56x ZeRO-3.
  - 14B: 122 TFLOPS, 8.13x and 12.20x.
  - 32B: 114 TFLOPS; both baselines run out of memory.
- **Consumer cards (Table 9; sequence 8,192, checkpoint every 4 layers).**

## 5. Relevance to Crowd
- **Stage size is limited by host RAM and PCIe, not VRAM.** In Table 9, a 24 GB card with about 100 GB of RAM trained
  a 14B model alone. For a 27B model that could mean 2-4 stages on well-equipped volunteers instead of dozens of
  VRAM-sized stages. Fewer stages means fewer WAN hops, and Agora (2607.13332) found per-hop latency to be the
  throughput limiter.
- **The next step after Agora's CPU-side optimizer.** Agora already runs AdamW on the CPU and requires 80 GB of RAM.
  Streaming weights as well could let 8-12 GB cards serve big stages (untested).
- **A sizing rule for admission.** Measured host use on the 3090 is about 7.4-8 GB of RAM per billion parameters
  (Table 9); see the caveat below.
- **Complements SOLO** (2609.35440), which cuts activation memory per stage. Together they bound both weight and
  activation memory on small cards.
- **Helps forward-only designs from round 1** (SOMA 2609.37899, DUST). Streaming forward passes let a small card run
  the forward pass of a model larger than its VRAM.
- **Trust: no direct effect.** Layer-wise execution is deterministic in structure, so replay audits should stay
  feasible (unverified).

## 6. Caveats
- **Single GPU, single node.** No multi-GPU or network setting is tested.
- **Needs large host RAM.** Tests used 251 GB to 1.5 TB, while typical volunteer PCs have 16-64 GB.
- **Fine-tuning only.** The workload is supervised fine-tuning on MetaMathQA, not pretraining. Correctness is checked
  only by final accuracy at 7B and 14B.
- **Throughput depends on large batches** to hide PCIe transfers.
  - On the 3090, 14B runs at 30 TFLOPS, about 21% of the 142 TFLOPS peak listed in Table 8, and 341 tokens/s.
  - Each microbatch takes a long time, which conflicts with pipeline workers that serve small, latency-sensitive
    microbatches.
- **Internal inconsistencies:**
  - The checkpoint-interval ablation differs between Table 4 (batch 64, 240.45 TFLOPS) and the text (batch 32,
    184.2 TFLOPS).
  - The depth study's text says 10.9B to 43.0B parameters, but Table 5 starts at 7.62B for 28 layers.
  - Table 9's host RAM (about 7.4 bytes per parameter) is below the paper's own 12 bytes-per-parameter floor (§2.1),
    so the memory accounting is unclear (unverified).
- **"Full precision" means full-parameter BF16 mixed precision with FP32 Adam moments.** No FP32 master weights are
  described (unverified).
- **No SSD tier.** NVMe offload is left to future work.
