# 2604.10390 — LLM-PRISM: Characterizing Silent Data Corruption from Permanent GPU Faults in LLM Training (LLM-PRISM)

## 1. Citation

- **Title:** LLM-PRISM: Characterizing Silent Data Corruption from Permanent GPU Faults in LLM Training
  (PRISM = Permanent and Intermittent SDC Modeling)
- **Authors:** Abhishek Tyagi, Yuhao Zhu (University of Rochester); Saurabh Hukerikar, Nirmal Saxena, Yanxiang Huang,
  Philip Shirvani (NVIDIA); Chung-Hsuan Tung (Duke University)
- **Date:** arXiv v1, 12 Apr 2026; the text read is v2, 1 Sep 2026 (cs.AR). No venue stated.
- **Code:** no link in the paper.

## 2. Summary

Silent data corruption (SDC) is a hardware error that gives a wrong result without a crash or any error signal.
Earlier training studies injected one-off transient bit flips. This paper instead studies permanent and intermittent
faults (manufacturing escapes, early-life defects, aging) that keep corrupting one GPU for the rest of a run.

- **How:** error signatures come from register-transfer-level (RTL) simulation of the NVIDIA H100 design. They are
  replayed inside Megatron-LM over 7,664 full pretraining runs of GPT-2 124M and 355M in FP16, BF16 and FP8, about
  11,500 hours of training (Table I).
- **Mostly benign:** 57–76% of runs end at baseline quality. Faults that fire on ≤1% of iterations leave final PPL
  unchanged, although the weights already start to drift at 1%.
- **Four failure modes:** two of them are invisible to NaN checks. One, "gradual drift", even looks normal on the loss
  curve; only weight-space divergence exposes it.
- **Format matters:** FP8 outcomes are binary (recover or crash), while FP16 degrades silently the most.
- **Where it hits matters:** faults in forward activations or input gradients do far more damage than faults in weight
  gradients, which averaging across GPUs dilutes.

## 3. Method

- **RTL characterization (§III-B, Fig. 1).**
  - Stuck-at faults are injected into H100 functional units in proportion to their silicon area: FMA (fused
    multiply-add) paths, tensor cores, register files, L1/L2 caches and SRAM.
  - Over 6,000 error signatures were collected (bits hit, error size, spatial pattern). Only faults that evade on-chip
    RAS (reliability, availability, serviceability) protection are used (§IV-C).
  - Examples (Fig. 3): a tensor-core fault gives 3×3 patches sharing one flipped bit; an FMA output fault gives sporadic
    large outliers; an L1 cache-line fault corrupts whole rows in high-order bits.
- **Fault site (§III-C, Fig. 2).** Each fault is a 7-tuple:
  - rank (which GPU), fixed for the run;
  - layer and sub-module (attention, MLP, LayerNorm), sampled uniformly;
  - training phase: forward outputs, backward input gradients or backward weight gradients, sampled uniformly. This
    gives 2:1 backward:forward exposure;
  - start iteration;
  - error rate r;
  - fault density (fraction of a tile corrupted);
  - bit-flip profile drawn from the RTL signatures, with single-bit flips weighted more heavily.
- **Intermittency (§III-E).** A Bernoulli(r) draw per eligible iteration decides whether the defect fires. r=1.0 is a
  hard stuck-at defect; r≪1 is a marginal one. r is swept from 0.001 to 1.0 (Fig. 8). Its assumed distribution is built
  from prior reports, not measured here.
- **Assumption (§III-A).** At most one faulty GPU per run.
- **Setup (§IV).** GPT-2 Small and Medium trained from scratch on WikiText with Megatron-LM, on 16× H100 (2 nodes × 8).
  Data plus sequence parallelism, Adam, warmup + cosine schedule. 4,681 + 2,983 runs. The fault-free baseline PPL
  (perplexity) is 28.54. A run counts as "Changed" if PPL moves more than 1%, the fault-free run-to-run noise.

## 4. Key reported results

- **Four failure modes (RQ1, Fig. 5).**
  - Spike-and-recover: NaNs are absorbed by loss-scale halving and skipped steps.
  - Spike-and-degrade: frequent NaNs collapse the loss scale and the loss rises.
  - Silent degradation: no NaNs, but the loss settles above baseline.
  - Gradual drift: the loss looks like baseline but the weights drift; this comes mostly from backward-pass faults.
- **Weight divergence (Fig. 5, plot 2).** In every permanent-fault run, the L2 distance from the fault-free weights
  grows steadily and never rejoins the baseline. It stays near zero at r ≤ 0.005 and grows at r ≥ 0.01. In the
  paper's words: "loss alone can mask significant deviations".
- **Outcome mix (Fig. 6).**
  - Across formats, 57–76% of runs end unchanged.
  - GPT-2 Small FP16: 11.0% Changed, 16.7% crashed, 14.6% hit a NaN/Inf loss. About 60% of those NaN runs still finish,
    with elevated PPL.
  - BF16: 26.9% crashed, 6.3% silent change. FP8: 41.9% crashed, 0.5% silent change.
  - GPT-2 Medium FP16: 13.8% Changed. The larger model degrades silently more often.
- **Fault rate (Obsv. 1–2).** r = 0.001–0.01 is indistinguishable from baseline; degradation starts at r ≥ 0.05.
  Tail risk: FP16 at r=1.0 has median PPL 28.58 but a 95th percentile of 192.4.
- **Timing (Obsv. 3).** Faults starting at step 2,571 hurt more than those starting at 5,142 or 7,713. Early training
  is more fragile.
- **Phase (Obsv. 4).** Weight-gradient faults mostly self-correct: momentum smooths them and averaging over the 16
  GPUs divides them by N. Forward-output and input-gradient faults act before any cross-GPU averaging. They give the
  highest crash rates (about 60%) at matched fault rates, in every format.
- **Downstream (Fig. 7, Medium BF16).** Even spike-and-recover and gradual-drift models lose accuracy on the Children's
  Book Test and Winograd.
- **NaN/skip safeguard (RQ4, Fig. 9).** It turns many FP16 crashes into spike-and-recover. Non-diverged runs still
  reach about 600% PPL deviation in FP16 and about 400% in BF16. FP8 deviations stay under 1%.
- **Fleet context (cited, not measured here, §II-C).** Meta attributed 6 unplanned interruptions to SDC in a 54-day
  Llama 3 snapshot on 16K H100s. Google estimates one SDC event every week or two in Gemini training.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **What breaks at 1M GPUs.** SDC grows with device count. Scaling Meta's figure linearly (6 per 54 days at 16K GPUs)
  gives about 7 SDC-type events per day at 1M GPUs. Consumer cards (often no memory ECC, overclocked, hot rooms) may be
  worse. Both are our unverified assumptions. Crowd should assume some contributors are *silently wrong at all times*,
  separately from malice (bucket B6).
- **Validation design.** Loss-only scoring on a small batch can miss gradual drift; Covenant-72B's Gauntlet LossScore
  (2603.08163) is an example. This argues for two layers (our inference):
  1. cheap NaN/Inf and norm checks on every submission;
  2. replay audits on a random sample that compare the *update vector* (cosine or L2), as Crowd v1.5's audit did.
- **Less dilution in DiLoCo-style training.** Data parallelism averages a bad GPU's gradient errors by 1/N every step.
  With DiLoCo-style local steps (Covenant; Decoupled DiLoCo 2604.21428), a volunteer runs H inner steps alone, so
  corruption compounds before it is averaged over about 17 contributors per round (Covenant's average). The "weight
  gradients self-correct" result does not transfer directly, so bounded-influence aggregation matters more. An example
  is Covenant's median-norm rescaling. This is our inference.
- **Reputation for hardware.** A defective GPU has a fixed fault site and rate, so its bias repeats every round.
  Tracking each volunteer's agreement with the aggregate over time could flag it (inference). Rare activations
  (r ≤ 0.5%) barely move the weights, so one-off blips are not worth chasing.
- **Precision policy.** Avoid FP16 on volunteers. BF16 needs the NaN/skip safeguard plus audits. FP8's binary failures
  are the easiest to police; this composes with bucket B5's low-precision work but is untested at our scale.
- **Spend verification early.** Early training is the most fragile, so spend more audit and redundancy budget early
  in a run (inference).
- **Forward-only methods.** Zeroth-order and forward-only estimators (DUST; SOMA 2609.37899) put every SDC into the
  forward pass, the phase this paper found most harmful. Untested.
- **Local learning.** In SOLO-style block-local learning (2609.35440), a corrupted block may mainly corrupt itself.
  Untested.

## 6. Caveats

- Small models (124M/355M) on WikiText only; 1B–27B is untested (the authors' stated limitation).
- Datacenter H100 RTL only. Consumer GPUs are not modelled: GDDR memory, different RAS, overclocking and undervolting.
- One faulty GPU among 16. Many concurrent faulty devices, which is the million-GPU case, is not studied.
- Only synchronous data and sequence parallelism; DiLoCo local steps, pipeline and tensor parallelism are untested.
  The authors note that the parallel layout changes the blast radius.
- The rate distribution f(r) is constructed, not measured, so no fleet defect rate is given. Many per-rate values
  appear only in figures; we quote only numbers stated in the text.
