# 2604.11947 — ResBM: Residual Bottleneck Models for Low-Bandwidth Pipeline Parallelism (ResBM)

## 1. Citation
- **Authors:** Alan Aboudib (corresponding), Rodrigo Lopez Portillo A., Kalei Brady, Steffen Cruz. Macrocosmos AI.
- **Date:** arXiv v1, 13 Apr 2026 (cs.LG). Preprint; no venue stated.
- **Code:** none linked. The acknowledgments say ResBM is being integrated into IOTA, Macrocosmos's production
  framework for trustless decentralized training (not checked further).

## 2. Summary
Pipeline parallelism (PP) over consumer links needs roughly 100x or more compression of the activations and
activation gradients that cross stage boundaries. The prior state of the art, Subspace Models (SM; Ramasinghe et
al. 2025, the "Protocol Models" paper 2506.01260 that Agora builds on), reaches 100x but needs constrained
optimization: a modified AdamW plus Grassmann-manifold subspace updates. ResBM is an architecture change instead.
A small learned autoencoder sits at each pipeline boundary: the encoder on the sending device, the decoder on the
receiving device. It is trained end-to-end with an off-the-shelf optimizer, while a low-rank identity (skip) path
crosses the boundary. At 100x and 128x compression (bottlenecks of 40 and 32 dimensions from width 4096), a 2B
model trained with Muon matches an uncompressed AdamW baseline after 26B tokens. Over an 80 Mbit/s link it recovers
roughly datacenter throughput.

## 3. Method
- **Bottleneck (§3.2, §4.1).** Placed after the feed-forward network (FFN) of every block except the last, giving 7
  bottlenecks in an 8-block model.
  - Encoder `E_l`: two linear layers with a nonlinearity, mapping `z_l` (L x H) to `b_l` (L x h). Only `b_l` crosses
    the link.
  - Decoder `D_{l+1}`: same shape, on the next device; it rebuilds the activation before attention.
  - Bandwidth scales with h, not H. Compression 128x means h = 32; 100x means h = 40.
- **Identity path (§3.3, Eq. 2-6).** The generalized residual is `x_{l+1} = x_l P_id + F(x_l)`, where `P_id` is a
  rectangular identity that zero-pads or truncates. A chain of these is the identity on the first `min(c_i)`
  coordinates. Gradients therefore flow unimpeded along the h kept dimensions, but the residual stream crossing every
  boundary is h-dimensional.
- **Optimizer choice (§3.4, Fig. 1, App. B.1).**
  - AdamW collapses the rank of projection matrices, wasting a narrow bottleneck.
  - Muon (orthogonalized momentum updates via Newton-Schulz) keeps bottleneck weights higher-rank, so ResBM uses
    Muon.
  - The comparison baseline is deliberately AdamW, matching SM's protocol.
- **Setup (Table 1).** Llama-3-style 2B model: 8 blocks, hidden size 4096, vocabulary 50K, context 1024, batch 32,
  C4 data. Weight decay 0.01, peak learning rate 2e-4, warmup 500, cosine decay to 1% of peak (AdamW) or 10% (Muon).

## 4. Key reported results
- **vs SM (Fig. 2a-b, figure only).** ResBM beats the authors' reimplementation of SM at 100x and 128x under both
  optimizers, and Muon beats AdamW for compressed models.
- **26B C4 tokens, about 65% of the compute-optimal 40B (Table 2), final perplexity:**
  - AdamW uncompressed baseline: 21.75
  - ResBM with Muon at 100x: 21.60
  - ResBM with Muon at 128x: 21.77
- **Throughput (Table 3).** 8x A10G with GPipe, one block per GPU, batch 16, 12 hours of training; tokens/s and
  final loss:
  - Centralized at 10 Gbit/s: 7,530 tokens/s (loss 3.86).
  - Uncompressed at 80 Mbit/s: 609 tokens/s (loss 5.28).
  - ResBM at 80 Mbit/s: AdamW 100x 7,681 (4.10); Muon 100x 6,795 (4.03); AdamW 128x 7,837 (4.05); Muon 128x 6,795
    (3.98).
- **Gain vs uncompressed (App. A.2, Fig. A.2).** About 12.8x (AdamW) and 11.2x (Muon) at 80 Mbit/s; about 1x from
  800 Mbit/s to 10 Gbit/s.
- **Overhead (App. A.3).** +63.4M parameters (3.3%). Per boundary, 8 MiB falls to 64 KiB in bf16 for a 1,024-token
  sequence; per step, 112 MiB falls to 896 KiB across 7 boundaries.
- **Bottleneck effective rank at 128x (Table B.1).** Layer 1: Muon 19.95 of 32 vs AdamW 10.26. Mean over layers:
  13.96 vs 13.22.
- **Muon vs AdamW baselines (Fig. C.2).** The uncompressed Muon baseline beats the AdamW one. The paper notes that
  Muon-trained models are less compressible by low-rank schemes.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd
- **A simple primitive for model parallelism on low bandwidth.** It needs no special optimizer machinery, so it is
  simpler to build and audit than SSN (used by Agora, 2607.13332) or MAPL (2606.05484, considered in round 1). It
  directly serves "small cards hold chunks of a huge model."
- **Bandwidth stops being the PP bottleneck even on home uplinks** (our back-of-envelope, not from the paper):
  - At h = 32 in bf16, a body stage uploads about 128 bytes per token: 64 bytes of activation forward plus 64 bytes of
    gradient back.
  - That is about 19.5k tokens/s on a 20 Mbit/s uplink, more than an 8-24 GB card computes through a
    billion-parameter stage.
  - Latency and coordination remain the limits (Agora §5.10).
- **Cheap to audit.** Tiny boundary tensors make it cheap to hash, commit, store, or replay activations for
  spot-checking untrusted stages, and cheap to send a microbatch to two workers for redundancy. This is an idea, not
  tested here.
- **One optimizer for compression and asynchrony.** ResBM wants Muon, and 2606.30634 finds Muon robust to one-step
  pipeline delay.
- **Composes with local learning.** With SOLO (2609.35440), only forward activations cross boundaries, and ResBM
  makes them 32-dimensional.
- **Built for incentives.** It comes from the IOTA (Bittensor) team, so it was designed for a permissionless,
  incentivized network. Trust itself is not studied in this paper.

## 6. Caveats
- **Small, shallow tests.** 8 blocks of width 4096, context 1024, batch 32 sequences, C4 only. Perplexity only, with
  no downstream evaluations, and 26B tokens is below compute-optimal.
- **Baseline mismatch.** ResBM trains with Muon but is compared with an AdamW baseline. The stronger uncompressed
  Muon baseline (Fig. C.2) is never tabulated, so the true cost of compression against best practice is unverified.
- **Narrow residual stream.** A bottleneck after every block limits the residual stream to h dimensions at every
  boundary. Effects on deep stacks, long context, and retrieval-heavy skills are unknown. Placing bottlenecks only
  at stage boundaries, as deployment would, is untested.
- **Still behind after 12 hours.** All ResBM variants trail the centralized loss (3.98-4.10 vs 3.86; Table 3).
  Convergence claims come from single-GPU runs without real network transfer.
- **Narrow systems test.** One 8x A10G testbed running synchronous GPipe. How the 80 Mbit/s link was emulated is not
  described (unverified). No heterogeneity, churn, asynchrony, or Byzantine peers.
- **Self-built comparison.** The SM baseline is the authors' own reimplementation; no official code existed then.
