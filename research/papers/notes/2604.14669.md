# 2604.14669: Zeroth-Order Optimization at the Edge of Stability

## 1. Citation

- **Title:** Zeroth-Order Optimization at the Edge of Stability
- **Authors:** Minhak Song, Liang Zhang, Bingcong Li, Niao He, Michael Muehlebach, Sewoong Oh
  (KAIST / KRAFTON, ETH Zurich, MPI Intelligent Systems, University of Washington)
- **Venue / date:** ICML 2026 (PMLR 306); arXiv v2, 1 Jul 2026
- **arXiv:** 2604.14669 [cs.LG]

## 2. Summary

This is a theory paper. It does not propose a new optimizer. It asks when a zeroth-order (ZO) optimizer
built on the standard Gaussian two-point estimator is stable, and finds that the answer differs from
first-order (FO) training. FO gradient descent is stable when the step size is below
`2/lambda_max(H)`. ZO-GD is only *mean-square* stable when `eta` is roughly below `2/Tr(H)`. The
condition depends on the whole Hessian spectrum, and it can be far stricter. Momentum also works the
opposite way. With FO momentum the stability limit grows as `2(1+beta)/lambda_max`. With ZO-GDM it
*shrinks* as `2(1-beta)/Tr(H)`, because momentum also accumulates estimator noise. ZO-Adam's limit
depends on the preconditioned trace `Tr(P^-1 H)` and is largely independent of `beta1`. In full-batch
training of a CNN, ResNet-20, ViT, LSTM, and Mamba, `Tr(H_t)` rises and then settles just below the
threshold, which the authors call a "mean-square edge of stability". So large ZO step sizes implicitly
regularize the Hessian *trace*, not its top eigenvalue. Appendix B shows that averaging `n`
independent queries per step pushes the threshold back toward the FO limit `2/lambda_max` as `n`
grows.

## 3. Algorithm (estimators and optimizers analysed) and the stability rules

The objective is `min_x f(x)`, with `x in R^d`. In the analysis, `H = Hessian of f at a local min x*`
and its eigenvalues are `lambda_1 >= ... >= lambda_d >= 0`.

**Two-point estimator (Eq. 1):**
```
g_hat(x_t) = [ f(x_t + mu*u_t) - f(x_t - mu*u_t) ] / (2*mu) * u_t ,   u_t ~ N(0, I_d) iid
```
`mu` is the smoothing radius. Default `mu = 1e-3`. On a quadratic, `g_hat = u u^T H x`, which is unbiased.

**Optimizers:**
```
ZO-GD :  x_{t+1} = x_t - eta * g_hat(x_t)
ZO-GDM:  m_{t+1} = beta*m_t + g_hat(x_t);         x_{t+1} = x_t - eta*m_{t+1}        (Polyak, m_0=0)
ZO-Adam: m_{t+1} = beta1*m_t + (1-beta1)*g_hat
         v_{t+1} = beta2*v_t + (1-beta2)*g_hat**2
         P_{t+1} = (1-beta1^{t+1}) * [ diag( sqrt( v_{t+1}/(1-beta2^{t+1}) ) ) + eps*I ]
         x_{t+1} = x_t - eta * P_{t+1}^{-1} m_{t+1}            (equivalent to bias-corrected Adam)
Frozen ZO-Adam (theory only): P fixed.
```

**Exact mean-square critical step sizes** (linearized dynamics, Theorems 1-3):

- ZO-GD: `eta*_ms` is the unique `eta` with `eta*lambda_max < 1` and
  `sum_i eta*lambda_i / (2(1 - eta*lambda_i)) = 1`.
  Bounds: `2/(Tr H + 2 lambda_max) <= eta*_ms <= 2/Tr H`.
- ZO-GDM: `eta*lambda_max < 1 - beta^2` (as extracted) and
  `sum_i eta*lambda_i / ( 2(1-beta) (1 - eta*lambda_i/(1-beta^2)) ) = 1`.
  Bounds: `2(1-beta)/(Tr H + 2 lambda_max/(1+beta)) <= eta*_ms <= 2(1-beta)/Tr H`.
- Frozen ZO-Adam, assuming `PH = HP`, with `lambda~_i = eig(P^-1 H)`:
  `eta*lambda~_max < 1 + beta1` and `sum_i eta*lambda~_i / (2(1 - eta*lambda~_i/(1+beta1))) = 1`.
  Bounds: `2/(Tr(P^-1H) + 2 lambda~_max/(1+beta1)) <= eta*_ms <= 2/Tr(P^-1H)`.
- The *mean* stability threshold equals the FO one in every case (`2/lambda_max`,
  `2(1+beta)/lambda_max`, `2(1+beta1)/((1-beta1)lambda_max)`). The extra noise only shows up in the
  second moment.

**Covariance recursion** (the core tool; ZO-GD on `f = 0.5 x^T H x`):
```
Sigma_{t+1} = Sigma - eta(H Sigma + Sigma H) + eta^2 ( 2 H Sigma H + Tr(H Sigma H) I )
```
Mean-square stability holds iff the spectral radius of this operator is below 1. The `Tr(H Sigma H) I`
term is the rank-one global coupling that turns the trace into the governing quantity. For ZO-GDM the
per-eigendirection 2x2 block recursion is
`W_{i,t+1} = A_i W_i A_i^T + eta^2 (lambda_i^2 W_i11 + sum_j lambda_j^2 W_j11) Q`, with
`A_i = [[1-eta lambda_i, -beta],[eta lambda_i, beta]]` and `Q = [[1,-1],[-1,1]]`.
Krein-Rutman gives the exact spectral radius.

**Estimator variants (Appendix B):**

- *Forward (one-sided) difference* `(f(x+mu u) - f(x))/mu * u`. Same threshold, but the
  stable state has a nonzero covariance floor `Sigma_inf = (I - T_H)^-1 (eta^2 mu^2 Q_H)`, with
  `Q_H = 1/4 E[(u^T H u)^2 u u^T]`, i.e. O(mu^2) jitter at the minimum. The symmetric estimator has no floor.
- *Sphere directions* (radius sqrt d): the noise term is scaled by `d/(d+2)`. Slightly more stable,
  negligible at large d.
- *n queries averaged per step:*
  `T^(n)(Sigma) = Sigma - eta(H Sigma + Sigma H) + eta^2[(1+1/n) H Sigma H + (1/n) Tr(H Sigma H) I]`.
  As `n -> inf` this becomes FO GD, so `eta*_ms -> 2/lambda_max`.
  - **Our derivation, not in the paper:** repeating the Theorem-1 fixed-point argument with this
    operator gives the condition `sum_i eta*lambda_i / ( n (2 - eta(1+1/n) lambda_i) ) = 1`.
    That implies roughly `2n/(Tr H + (n+1) lambda_max) <= eta*_ms <= 2n/Tr H`. **Practical rule:**
    the stable LR grows about linearly in the number of averaged draws until it hits the FO limit.

**Implicit bias of mu** (Section 6, citing Zhang et al. 2025a): ZO approximately minimizes
`f_mu(x) = f(x) + (mu^2/2) Tr(Hessian f(x))`. With `mu >= 3e-3`, training never reached the EoS band
and stayed in a lower-trace regime.

**Curvature measurement used:** every 1,000 iterations they measure `lambda_max` by power iteration
(50 iterations of Hessian-vector products, HVPs) and `Tr(H)` with Hutchinson's estimator
(500 probes). For Adam they use `P_t^-1 H_t`, and they check commutativity with
`||[P,H]||_F/||PH||_F` (50 Rademacher probes, 2 HVPs each).

**Experimental hyperparameters (Appendix C):**

- Data and loss: CIFAR-10 subset, 1,000 examples from 4 classes, squared loss on one-hot targets,
  full batch, constant step size, `mu = 1e-3`, about 5e5 iterations. Models: a 4-layer CNN (width 32,
  GeLU, average pooling), ResNet-20 with GroupNorm, and a ViT (depth 3, dim 64, 8 heads, MLP 256,
  patch 4). Sequence tasks: LSTM and Mamba on the minGPT sort task (digits 1-4, length 8; 1,000 or
  250 examples). Single H100.
- Example step sizes: ZO-GD `eta in {2/5000, 2/4000, 2/3000}` (FO GD used `2/400..2/200`, so ZO's
  stable `eta` is about 10-20x smaller). ZO-GDM: `eta = 1e-4`, `beta in {0.8, 0.85, 0.9}`. ZO-Adam:
  `(beta1, beta2) = (0.9, 0.999)`, `eta in {2/40000, 2/20000, 2/10000}`.

## 4. Key reported results

- **Table 1 (stability conditions):**
  - GD: `2/lambda_max`. GDM: `2(1+beta)/lambda_max`. Adam: `2(1+beta1)/((1-beta1) lambda_max(P^-1H))`.
  - ZO-GD: `[2/(Tr+2lambda_max), 2/Tr]`.
  - ZO-GDM: `[2(1-beta)/(Tr + 2lambda_max/(1+beta)), 2(1-beta)/Tr]`.
  - ZO-Adam: `[2/(Tr~ + 2lambda~_max/(1+beta1)), 2/Tr~]`.
- **Fig. 1:** for GD, `lambda_max(H_t)` settles at `2/eta`. For ZO-GD, `Tr(H_t)` settles slightly
  below `2/eta` (e.g. about 3000-5000 for `eta = 2/3000..2/5000`). `lambda_max` is not the governing
  quantity.
- **Figs. 2, 6, 7, 8, 9 (CNN, ResNet, ViT, LSTM, Mamba):** in all three ZO optimizers, the threshold
  stays inside `[Tr, Tr + 2lambda_max/(...)]` for most of training after progressive sharpening.
- **Fig. 3 (catapults):** raising `eta` mid-run produces a loss spike. `Tr(H)` drops sharply, then
  re-equilibrates near the new `2/eta`.
- **Fig. 4 (mu):** with `mu <= 1e-3` the run sits at EoS. With `mu >= 3e-3` the trace saturates well
  below `2/eta`.
- **Fig. 5:** mini-batch ZO-SGD (batch 500, 250) trains in flatter, lower-trace regimes than full batch.
- **App. D.2/D.3:** the ZO-Adam threshold is insensitive to `beta1 in {0.1, 0.5, 0.9}`. The commutator
  ratio falls from 0.8-0.9 to below 0.05 during training.
- **Not reported:** no LLM experiments and no wall-clock or accuracy-vs-baseline comparisons. The
  paper is diagnostic.

## 5. Classification for Crowd v1.5

**Complement to DUST.** It is analysis and tuning guidance, not a new estimator.

- **Main DUST shortcoming addressed: instability and divergence (LR and sigma choice).**
  - It predicts the safe step size from measurable curvature: `eta < ~2(1-beta) n_eff / Tr(H_eff)`.
  - It explains why heavy server momentum (DUST uses SGD with `momentum=0.9`) gives no stability
    benefit for ZO. `beta = 0.9` shrinks the stable raw LR 10x. In effective-LR terms
    (`eta/(1-beta)`) the limit stays at `~2/Tr`, whereas FO momentum would buy `(1+beta)/(1-beta)`.
    So momentum tuned by FO intuition overshoots.
  - It explains why larger sigma acts as a trace regularizer. Under relative sigma, the effective
    `mu` changes over training as activation RMS changes, so the implicit regularizer and the noise
    floor drift.
- **Secondary: variance and the number of draws.** The n-query result says that doubling the
  population (antithetic draws, summed across all clients in a round) roughly doubles the stable LR
  until it reaches the backprop limit. The aggregation size at the coordinator therefore matters
  directly for the usable LR.
- **Mapping caveat:** DUST perturbs layer *outputs*, not parameters, and turns the loss differences
  into weight gradients via outer products with recorded inputs. The relevant "H" is then the Hessian
  w.r.t. the perturbed activations, as seen through that outer-product map, not the parameter
  Hessian. The qualitative laws (trace-governed, momentum harmful, ~n scaling) should carry over, but
  the constants need measuring.
- **Does not address:** communication bytes, Byzantine peers, memory.

## 6. MVP recipe (2-10 min, M5 Max, MPS)

**Goal:** check whether the paper's stability laws predict DUST's divergence boundary on our 10M GPT
(preset `10m`: 6 layers, d=256, vocab 4096, seq 128), and whether a trace-aware LR beats the best
fixed LR.

**Pieces to build** (as `research/lab/methods/zo_eos.py` plus a small probe script; reuse
`harness.py` and `crowd_v15.dust`):

1. **Trace estimator for the parameter Hessian.** Hutchinson with Rademacher `z`: `z^T H z` via
   `torch.autograd.grad(g, params, z)` on a fixed probe batch (B=8, T=128), with 20-50 probes. Use
   power iteration (20 iterations) for `lambda_max`. Run on MPS in float32. Check that double
   backward works on MPS; fall back to CPU if it does not.
2. **Activation-space trace for DUST** (closer to what DUST actually perturbs). For each perturbed
   linear output `y_l`, compute Hutchinson `Tr(d^2 L / d y_l^2)` with HVPs w.r.t. the hooked
   activations, then sum over layers. Weight it by `sigma_l^2` if relative sigma is used, since the
   effective perturbation is `sigma_l * eps`.
3. **ZO-SGD (MeZO-style parameter perturbation) arm** with `n` antithetic draws:
   `g = (1/n) sum_k [(L(x+mu u_k) - L(x-mu u_k))/(2mu)] u_k`, `mu = 1e-3`, seeded `u` (regenerate
   from the seed, never store it).
4. **LR sweep harness:** for each `(method in {ZO-SGD, DUST}, beta in {0, 0.9}, n in {4, 16, 64})`,
   sweep LR on a log grid (8 points). Run 300-600 steps each, fixed batch. Record "diverged" if the
   train loss exceeds `1.5x` its initial value or is NaN. This takes about 1-2 minutes per cell on
   the 10m preset with small n; trim the grid to fit.
5. **Trace-aware LR controller:**
   - `eta_t = c * 2 (1-beta) n / Tr_hat_t`, with `c in {0.3, 0.5}`.
   - Re-estimate `Tr_hat` every 50 steps with 10 Hutchinson probes, EMA-smoothed.
   - Compare with the best fixed LR from step 4 and with Adam backprop (`methods/adam.py`).

**Metrics:**

- Measured divergence LR versus predicted `2(1-beta) n / Tr(H)` (and the lower bound with
  `lambda_max`).
- Loss vs tokens and loss vs wall time for: Adam-BP, DUST (`methods/dust.py` defaults
  `pop=32, sigma_rel=0.05, momentum=0.9`), DUST with `beta=0`, and DUST with the trace-aware LR.
- Logged `Tr(H_t)` vs `2(1-beta)n/eta` over time: does DUST sit at a "mean-square EoS"?

**Proves out if:**

1. The empirical divergence LR lies within about 2x of the predicted band for both `beta = 0` and
   `beta = 0.9`. For `beta = 0.9`, the raw `eta` at divergence should be about 0.1x the `beta = 0`
   value. Equivalently, the *effective* LR `eta/(1-beta)` should diverge at the *same* place
   (`~2/Tr`) with or without momentum, whereas FO-momentum would gain a factor `(1+beta)/(1-beta)`.
   So compare `eta/(1-beta)` across arms.
2. The stable LR scales about linearly with `n` from 4 to 64.
3. The trace-aware schedule matches or beats the best fixed LR on loss-at-budget without a sweep, and
   never diverges.

If (1) fails for DUST but holds for parameter-space ZO-SGD, the activation-space mapping is the
culprit; try the activation-trace estimate from step 2 instead.

**Cheap extra:** a catapult test. Double the LR mid-run and check for a loss spike plus a `Tr(H)` drop.
That reproduces Fig. 3 on an LM in under 2 minutes.

## 7. Caveats and unclear points

- **The theory is local.** It covers a linearized quadratic near a minimum and *full-batch*
  training. Mini-batch ZO-SGD, our regime, is only shown empirically to sit at *lower* curvature
  (Fig. 5). No theory combines data noise with direction noise.
- **No LLM or transformer-LM pretraining experiments.** The models are tiny (a 1,000-example CIFAR
  subset with squared loss), and the runs are about 5e5 iterations. Cross-entropy LM training
  dynamics may differ.
- **ZO-GDM condition:** the extracted text reads `eta lambda_max < 1 - beta^2` and the term
  `(1 - eta lambda_i/(1-beta^2))`. PDF extraction could have garbled `(1-beta)^2`. The bounds
  `2(1-beta)/Tr` are unambiguous; check the PDF before coding the exact root-finder.
- **ZO-Adam theory assumes `P H = H P`,** which is only empirically approximate (commutator below
  0.05 after warmup).
- **The paper does not convert the results into an LR rule or controller.** The trace-aware LR above
  is our extrapolation. The n-query bound formula above is our derivation from their Appendix B
  operator.
- **DUST's estimator differs from theirs** in three ways: activation-space perturbation, temporal
  reward discounting (`gamma`), and per-layer relative sigma. The quantitative thresholds need
  empirical recalibration, though the qualitative laws (trace, momentum, n) are what matter.
- **Hutchinson trace estimates on a 10M model are noisy** with few probes. Use 20 or more probes for
  the boundary test.
- **Implicit trace regularization is an observation.** The paper does not claim it helps
  generalization or LM perplexity.
