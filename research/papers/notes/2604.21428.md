## 2. Summary

Decoupled DiLoCo splits training across M independent **learners**. Each learner runs inner AdamW steps
nonstop and never waits for its peers. A central CPU-only **syncer** keeps the global parameters and the outer
optimizer state. As in Streaming DiLoCo, the model is split into P fragments, and each fragment is synced every
H steps at its own offset, so one fragment is in flight every step. The syncer waits only for a **minimum
quorum K** of learners (default K=1). It extends the wait with an **adaptive grace window** that fits inside the
communication slack. It merges the fragments it receives with **token/step-based weights** and **Radial-Directional
Averaging (RDA)**, which averages norms and directions separately. It then applies the outer optimizer (SGD
Nesterov) and broadcasts the updated fragment. Learners overwrite their local copy of the fragment when the
update arrives. Under heavy simulated chip failures (1.2M simulated chips, MTBI 1 year per chip), M=8 keeps 88%
goodput (elastic DP: 58%) with no loss in downstream quality. Results are shown for 2B–9B dense and 2.8B/3.8B-active
MoE models, plus heterogeneous TPU generations and opportunistic "scavenged" compute.

## 3. Algorithm

### 3.1 Symbols
- `M`: number of learners (default experiments: M=8).
- `P`: number of fragments. `H`: steps between syncs of the same fragment. `t_p ∈ {0..H−1}`: fragment p's offset.
  Defaults: P=H=24, so one fragment is sent per step.
- `τ`: overlap. A fragment sent at step t comes back τ steps later. Default τ=2, so two fragments are in flight.
- `θ_{m,p}`: learner m's local copy of fragment p. `Θ_p`: the syncer's global copy of fragment p.
- `c^steps_{m,p}`, `c^tokens_{m,p}`: steps and tokens learner m has processed since it last received fragment p.
- `t_m`: learner m's local step. `t`: global (syncer) step, sent back to learners.
- `K`: minimum quorum (default K=1). `M_t`: set of learners that reported in time.
- `ξ_step, ξ_quorum, ξ_sync`: durations of a compute step, of gathering the quorum, and of the sync, tracked
  with EMAs. `γ<1`: safety margin.
- `InnerOpt`: AdamW. `OuterOpt`: SGD with Nesterov momentum. **This paper does not give the outer lr or momentum
  values.**

### 3.2 Learner (Alg. 1)
```
θ_m ← Θ(0);  c_steps[p]=c_tokens[p]=0 ∀p;  t_m=0; t=0
while t ≤ T:
    t_m += 1; x ~ D_m
    for p: c_steps[p] += 1; c_tokens[p] += |x|
    θ_m ← AdamW(θ_m, ∇f(x, θ_m))
    send_async(syncer, t_m, c_steps, c_tokens)            # metadata only, non-blocking
    for (Θ_p, t_s) in recv_all_pending(syncer):
        θ_{m,p} ← Θ_p           # overwrite (mixing α=0; Eq.5 θ←αθ+(1−α)Θ, α=0 best for M≥8)
        c_steps[p]=c_tokens[p]=0
        t = t_s
```
After each step, the learner copies its parameters to host RAM. The syncer pulls fragments from that copy, so
no extra HBM copy is needed.

### 3.3 Syncer (Alg. 2)
```
for t = 1..T:
  if ∃p: t mod H == t_p:
     U_t ← recv(learners, at least K)  + wait up to ξ_grace for more
     for m in M_t: θ_{m,p} ← pull(learner m)
     w_{m,p} = Weight(c_tokens_{m,p}, c_steps_{m,p})
     Δ_p = Merge({θ_{m,p}, w_{m,p}}, Θ_p^{(t−H)})
     Θ_p^{(t)} = OuterOpt(Θ_p^{(t−H)}, Δ_p)     # SGD-Nesterov
     broadcast(Θ_p^{(t)}, t)
```
- **Per-learner outer gradient:** `Δ_{m,p} = Θ_p^{(t−H)} − θ_{m,p}^{(t)}`.
- **Weight:** the extracted text reads `Weight = c_tokens × (c_tokens / c_steps)`, described as
  "(quantity) × (quality)". The fraction typesetting is ambiguous; see §7.
- **Weighted direct average:** `Δ_p = Σ w_m Δ_{m,p} / Σ w_m`.
- **RDA (used on non-embedding fragments):** with `φ(x) = x/||x||_2`,
  `RDA({(v_i,w_i)}) = (Σ w_i ||v_i|| / Σ w_i) · φ( Σ w_i φ(v_i) / Σ w_i )`.
  The result has the average norm in the average direction. This is needed because per-learner outer gradients
  are almost pairwise orthogonal, so a plain mean has norm about R/√M, and the outer LR would need retuning
  for every M.
- **Final choice:** RDA on the non-embedding parameters, plain (weighted) average on the embedding. The
  embedding's outer gradients are *not* near-orthogonal.
- **Absent learner:** its weight is 0. Its syncer shard persists, so the syncer needs no reconfiguration.

### 3.4 Adaptive grace window (Eq. 3)
`ξ_slack = τ·ξ_step − (ξ_quorum + ξ_sync)`, and `ξ_grace ≤ γ·ξ_slack`. After K learners have reported, the
syncer keeps collecting for ξ_grace so that more learners are included without stalling anyone. In the default
setup, the grace window lasts at most about one step. The paper reports that the grace window works better
than simply raising K when learner speeds differ.

### 3.5 Fragmentation (App. C)
**Balanced tensor fragmentation** is greedy number partitioning (LPT). Sort tensors by size in descending
order and put each one into the currently smallest bin, for P bins. Its peak is at most 4/3 of the optimal
peak. The embedding sits alone in a fragment, which is much larger. Layer, tensor, and balanced strategies give
similar quality (Table 7); balanced is used because it minimizes peak bandwidth.

### 3.6 Other knobs
- **Outer-gradient compression:** int4 costs +0.09% relative val loss. 2-bit costs +32%, and 1-bit costs +83%
  with near-collapse on vision (Table 10). No error feedback is used here.
- **Recovery:** a new or rejoining learner gets params and inner-optimizer state from a healthy peer, keyed on
  the syncer's vector clock `t_s`. It buffers fragment updates during the transfer and replays them. Recovery
  is bounded to H syncer steps.
- **Determinism:** event tapes (vector clocks, token counters, failures) allow bitwise replay.
  Chandy–Lamport snapshots provide consistent checkpoints.
- **Chaos model:** per-step chip failures are Poisson with rate `N_chip/MTBI_chip`. Downtime follows an
  exponentiated Weibull distribution. Reconfiguration time is fixed. The batch is scaled down proportionally
  when slices fail.

## 4. Key reported results

- **Goodput (Table 1a, MTBI 1 year):** at 1.2M chips, elastic DP (M=1) gets 58%, M=8 gets 88%, and M=16 gets 93%.
  At 2.4M chips, elastic DP gets 40% and M=16 gets 86%. Uptime is about 99–100% for M≥4.
  Rule of thumb: goodput(M, N) ≈ goodput(kM, kN).
- **5B dense, 1T tokens (Table 2):** Text average: DP 70.1 (no failures) vs. Decoupled M=8 69.8 (no failures)
  vs. 69.8 at 1.2M chips (goodput 88%). Vision averages are 58.7 / 58.8 / 58.6.
- **MoE 2.8B active, 170B tokens (Table 3):** Text: DP 68.4 vs. Decoupled 67.9 (no failures) and 68.4 (with
  failures).
- **Scale (Table 14), DP vs. Decoupled text average:** 2B 52.3/51.6, 5B 62.5/62.8, 9B 67.8/68.0.
- **Token budget (Table 12, 2B), DP vs. Decoupled text average:** 26B 55.1/54.4, 260B 61.8/62.1, 1.3T 64.5/64.9.
  Decoupled trails at low token budgets and leads at high ones.
- **Heterogeneous hardware (Table 5, 2B, TPUv5e/v5p mix, 18%+10% speed spread):**
  | Setting | Goodput | Text avg | Vision avg |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | K=8 (sync) | 84% | 54.4 | 37.6 |
  | K=1, no grace | 100% | 51.4 | 25.7 |
  | K=1 + grace | 100% | 55.3 | 37.0 |
  Pure K=1 without grace is clearly harmful. The grace window recovers synchronous quality.
- **Merge ablation (Tables 8–9, 2B):** at M=16, Avg/Avg gives text 52.7 and vision 28.2, while RDA(model)/Avg(embed)
  gives 54.5 and 33.6. At M=8 the differences are small.
- **M sweep with failures (Table 11, 2B):** text is neutral from M=2 to 8 and slightly worse at M=16.
- **Bandwidth (Table 13, 5B, 1 s step, 2 DCs, Gbit/s needed for 90% compute utilization):** DP 104,
  Decoupled bf16 1.7, Decoupled int4 0.43.
- **Scavenging (Table 4, iso-FLOPs, 2B):** +300% extra learners for half the run gives train time 0.62× with
  no quality change. DP gets 0.80×.
- **Geo-distributed real run:** 12B model, M=8 across US regions. Step time is about the same as the collocated
  run; DP is more than 10–20× slower.

## 5. Classification for Crowd v1.5

**Complement to DUST.** It is a coordinator and outer-loop design, not a gradient estimator.

- **Async / stragglers / heterogeneity / churn:** this is the strongest fit. K-quorum plus grace window,
  per-fragment streaming, token-weighted merges, and the non-blocking learner loop all map onto a coordinator
  serving thousands of consumer GPUs with very different speeds and frequent dropouts.
- **Communication bytes / peak bandwidth:** fragment streaming means 1/P of the model per step instead of the
  full model every H steps. The int4 result (with no EF) shows how far plain quantization goes. SparseLoCo-style
  EF is needed below 4 bits.
- **Low gradient cosine / high variance:** RDA targets the norm shrinkage that comes from averaging
  near-orthogonal updates. DUST client estimates are also near-orthogonal and noisy. **Warning:** for ZO
  estimates, most of each vector's norm is noise. RDA would keep the *noisy* norm while averaging away the
  direction, which inflates step size relative to signal. Test this; do not adopt it blindly.
- **Byzantine / untrusted:** **not addressed.** Learners are trusted. `c_tokens` and `c_steps` are
  self-reported, so a liar can inflate its weight. This needs Gauntlet- or Throttle-style defenses.
- **Memory on consumer GPUs:** not addressed. The syncer is CPU-only and its state stays off the GPU, which is
  compatible with our coordinator.
- **Hybrid idea:** a learner could run H DUST steps instead of H AdamW steps and send fragment deltas.
  Decoupled DiLoCo supplies the asynchronous outer loop around DUST.

## 6. MVP recipe (M5 Max, MPS, PyTorch 2.15, about 2–10 min)

**Setup:** a 7M-parameter GPT (vocab 4096, seq 128, FineWeb-Edu). Simulate M=4–8 learners in a single process
on a **virtual clock**. Learner m's step time is `base·s_m·(1+ε)`, with `s_m` drawn from {1, 1.2, 2, 5} for
heterogeneity and ε about U(0, 0.1). Inject a dropout tape: learner m is offline for steps [a, b). Then run an
event loop that always advances the learner with the earliest next-event time. Keep one parameter copy per
learner. For 7M parameters that is 8 copies plus AdamW state, about 0.7 GB, which fits.

**Minimal pieces:**
1. `fragments = greedy_lpt(model.named_parameters(), P)`, with the embedding alone. For a small model,
   P=H≈8–12, since there are only about 30–60 tensors.
2. Learner state: params, AdamW, `c_steps[p]`, `c_tokens[p]`, and a pending-update queue that delivers τ=2
   steps later.
3. Syncer: global Θ, per-fragment Nesterov momentum buffer, quorum K, and a grace window in virtual time
   (wait up to `γ·(τ·ξ_step − ξ_sync)`; γ=0.5 is our choice).
4. Merge options: `avg`, `weighted_avg`, and `rda` (for non-embedding). Weight `w = c_tokens²/c_steps`, as
   literally extracted; also try `w = c_tokens`.
5. Outer optimizer: SGD-Nesterov. **Hyperparameters are not in this paper.** Start from the original
   DiLoCo defaults (lr 0.7, μ 0.9; these come from prior work, not this paper) and sweep lr in {0.4, 0.7, 1.0}.

**Arms:**
- `adam` (DP-equivalent batch)
- `sync_streaming_diloco` (K=M)
- `decoupled` (K=1 + grace)
- `decoupled_no_grace` (K=1)
- `dust` (current coordinator)
- `decoupled_dust`: learners take DUST steps as the inner optimizer

**Metrics:**
- val loss vs. tokens
- val loss vs. *virtual* wall time
- goodput (fraction of learner-time that produced merged work)
- peak bytes per step
- mean |M_t| per merge

**"Proves out" if:**
1. Under a straggler and dropout tape, `decoupled` reaches `sync_streaming_diloco`'s final loss in
   noticeably less virtual time, with ≤1–2% loss gap at equal tokens.
2. `decoupled_no_grace` is measurably worse than with grace, reproducing Table 5's direction.
3. At M=8, RDA ≥ avg. Report whether RDA helps or hurts for `decoupled_dust`, given the warning in §5.

## 7. Caveats / unclear points

- **Outer optimizer hyperparameters are missing:** no outer lr or momentum, no inner lr or batch size, no H
  per scale beyond P=H=24.
- The **Weight formula** is ambiguous in the extraction. It reads as `c_tokens × (c_tokens / c_steps)`,
  i.e. tokens²/steps. The "quality = amortized over fewer steps" wording could also mean
  `c_tokens × (1/c_steps)` or a normalized form. Verify against the PDF before relying on it.
- With α=0 overwrite, a learner's local progress on fragment p during the τ in-flight steps is discarded. The
  impact is not quantified beyond the α ablation (D.1).
- Hardware failures are *simulated*: real ML runs on O(1k) chips with the batch scaled down to imitate O(1M).
  Failure statistics (MTBI 1 year, Weibull downtime) are chosen arbitrarily.
- Learners are large TPU slices with identical data distributions. There is no non-IID data, no Byzantine
  actors, and no consumer-grade network. The geo-distributed run is one experiment, reported only as a
  normalized step time.
- Downstream differences are within an "ambient noise floor". The paper reports no seeds or error bars.
- K=1 without grace hurts badly (vision 37.6 → 25.7). Sync frequency and quorum size matter more than the
  headline suggests.
- int4 holds up, but 2-bit and below fail **without EF**. This contrasts with SparseLoCo's 2-bit **with** EF,
  so the methods are not directly comparable.
