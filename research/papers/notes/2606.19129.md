## 2. Summary

Robust aggregation rules (median, trimmed mean) must look at individual updates, while privacy wants to
hide them. Earlier designs run robust aggregation inside secure multi-party computation (MPC) with
all-to-all (A2A) or all-to-one-committee (A2C) topologies, where per-party cost grows at least linearly with
the number of parties n. Giskard does three things:

1. It rewrites the coordinate-wise median as a binary search. Each step only needs a count of parties
   whose value is at or below a public pivot. Each party contributes one bit per coordinate and must prove
   it is 0 or 1, so a Byzantine party can move each count by at most one.
2. It sums those bits up a k-ary tree of small committees (size O(log n)) drawn from a public random seed,
   using secret sharing inside each committee.
3. It adds distributed differential-privacy (DP) noise at the root.

Per-party communication becomes polylogarithmic in n. Costs are modeled up to n = 10^6 parties.

## 3. Method

- **Median by counting** (Algorithm 1): Niter = ⌈log2(2u/q)⌉ binary-search steps over the value range
  (−u, u] at precision q. An honest party sends b = 1[x ≤ pivot]. The root reveals count + Gaussian noise,
  N(0, Niter/2ρ), for a zero-concentrated DP budget ρ. The Byzantine bias on each count lies in [0, f]
  (§IV-A).
- **Committee tree** (Algorithm 2, App. B-A): a one-time seed agreement via scalable Byzantine agreement
  with polylog cost (Lemma 1). Per tree level, a Fisher–Yates permutation seeded by H(seed‖level) forms
  committees, so each party sits in at most one committee per level. Committee size m = O(ε⁻² ln n) keeps
  fewer than m/4 corrupted members in every committee with probability 1 − n^−c, provided at most
  (1/4 − ε)n parties are corrupted (Theorem 5).
- **Per round:** leaves secret-share their bit vectors to a base committee using BGW verifiable secret
  sharing (BGW = Ben-Or–Goldwasser–Wigderson). The committee checks each value is a bit
  (MULTIPLY(b, 1−b) = 0) and replaces invalid values with zero, then sums. RESHARE passes partial sums up
  the tree. The root adds jointly generated noise (NOISEGEN), reveals the noisy count and broadcasts the
  next pivot down; each child accepts a value sent by more than m/4 of its parent's members.
- **Assumptions** (§III): f < n/4, a synchronous network, a public-key infrastructure, a static adversary.
- **Which robust rules can be distributed this way** (§IV-D, Table I): G1, compact mergeable summaries
  with few public query rounds; G2, bounded inputs that are cheap to certify; G3, low secure-arithmetic
  cost. Bit- or sign-valued rules pass (Giskard's quantile search, signSGD majority vote, ℓ1-RSA, BRACE);
  Krum, a direct median or trimmed mean, and the geometric median fail.

## 4. Key reported results

- **Robustness bound** (Theorem 3): |M̂ − x̄_H| ≤ √κ0·σ_H + q/2, with κ0 = (n−f)/(⌈n/2⌉−f). This meets
  (f, κ)-robustness up to a discretization term Dq²/2. The median tolerates f < n/2; the protocol needs
  f < n/4.
- **Asymptotic communication** (Theorem 6, Table II): per party O(d·Niter·log³n) in the optimistic case
  (log⁵n with complaints), versus O(d·n·log²n) for A2C and O(d·n²) for A2A. d is the model dimension.
- **Measured cost model** (§VI-A, Fig. 4): field elements counted from the Rust implementation, for **one
  scalar coordinate** with Niter = 10.
  - Per-party cost at f/n = 0.10 grows from 392 GB at n = 10³ to 1.99 TB at n = 10⁶. That is 5.1× for a
    10³× larger network, versus about 572× for A2C and 1.00×10⁹× for A2A.
  - At n = 10⁶, per-party cost is 146 GB to 3.09 PB across f/n ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}, versus 45.3 TB to
    44.4 PB for A2C. At f/n = 0.01, Giskard uses 722× less than A2C.
  - At n = 10⁶ and f/n = 0.10, root-committee noise generation is 89.5% of Giskard's per-party cost.
- **Latency model** (Fig. 5; 1 Gbps per party, 1 s round trip, f/n = 0.10): Giskard takes minutes to hours
  across the tested n. A2C takes multiple hours to days from n = 10⁵. A2A is impractical already at 10⁴.
- **Utility, plaintext emulation** (Table III; n = 100, f = 25, attacks with white-box access: Fall of
  Empires (FoE) and A Little Is Enough (ALIE)). In the near-IID case (independent and identically
  distributed data, Dirichlet α = 100), Giskard's median reaches 0.97–0.98 on MNIST. On CIFAR-10 it scores
  0.57 (FoE) and 0.48 (ALIE), trimmed mean 0.48 and 0.43, and CAF (an alternative robust rule) 0.70. Under
  severe non-IID data (α = 0.1), MNIST under FoE falls to 0.18 versus 0.97 for CAF: median-type rules
  collapse when data is heterogeneous.
- **DP** (Fig. 6; EMNIST, n = 11,850, f = 24%): Giskard is best in 11 of 12 settings. Under sign flipping
  it stays near 91% at every budget. Under FoE at ε = 100 it reaches 63.1%, versus 16.0% for local DP + CAF
  and 10% for local DP + median.
- **Committee size** (Fig. 3, failure probability 2⁻⁴⁰): stable in n for f/n ≤ 1/5. Exact values appear
  only in the figure and could not be read from the text.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **The only 2026 Byzantine-robust aggregation protocol we found that is costed at n = 10⁶**, which is the
  owner's "millions of GPUs" question. Its answer has two parts.
- **Negative: confidential (MPC) robust aggregation does not fit LLM-sized updates.** Cost is per
  coordinate: at least 146 GB per party for ONE scalar at n = 10⁶ (Fig. 4). A 1B-parameter update
  multiplies that by about 10⁹. Crowd also does not need confidentiality, because contributions are meant to
  be public so that anyone can verify them.
- **Positive: two structural ideas carry over in cleartext** (our analysis):
  1. Beacon-seeded committee trees. Random committees per level, each party in at most L committees, and
     failure bounded by Chernoff plus a union bound. This is a concrete way to scale orchestrator and
     verifier roles beyond the ~100–200-validator Byzantine-fault-tolerant (BFT) set in our consensus draft
     (03-consensus-and-record).
  2. Bounded-influence contributions. If each peer contributes a validated bit or sign per coordinate, one
     Sybil moves each count by at most one, so robustness becomes a counting argument. In cleartext the
     per-peer upload is d·Niter bits per round: 1B parameters × 10 iterations ≈ 1.25 GB. That is too much
     for a 20 Mbit/s uplink, unless it is applied only to compressed (top-k) coordinates or per-chunk
     statistics.
- **Committee sizing** (our arithmetic, not from the paper: binomial tail plus a union bound over n/m
  committees, failure probability 2⁻⁴⁰). At n = 10⁶ with a Sybil share f/n = 10%, a committee needs about
  353 members to keep corrupted members under 1/4, but only about 67 to keep them under 1/2 (honest-majority
  voting). At f/n = 20% the figures are about 4,085 and 151. Majority-vote verifier committees (as in Chorus,
  2609.37367) are therefore cheap at million scale. Committees that must stay below 1/4 corrupted (Giskard's
  MPC requirement; BFT consensus needs below 1/3) need hundreds of members at a 10% Sybil share and
  thousands at 20%. **The real scaling lever is the cost of a Sybil identity** (probation, caps, reputation
  from verified work).
- **G1–G3 as a design screen:** sign or majority-vote aggregation suits SparseLoCo-style sign or 2-bit
  values (Covenant-72B, 2603.08163) better than Krum or geometric-median rules.
- **Round-1 links:** Soft Throttling (2609.39357) bounds Byzantine influence in asynchronous SGD at a single
  server, and Decoupled DiLoCo (2604.21428) uses one syncer with a quorum. Giskard removes the single
  aggregator at polylog cost, but it assumes synchronous rounds, the opposite of Decoupled DiLoCo.
- **Heterogeneity warning** (Table III): a coordinate-wise median is acceptable for Crowd's IID,
  beacon-assigned shards. It is risky in any lane where peers choose their own data.

## 6. Caveats

- No language-model experiments. Robustness is tested on MNIST and CIFAR-10 at n = 100, and DP on EMNIST at
  n = 11,850, all in plaintext emulation. The 10⁶ figure comes from a cost model calibrated on the Rust
  code, not from a training run with 10⁶ parties (§VI-A, §VI-B).
- Static adversary, synchronous network, a public-key infrastructure, f < n/4. Churn and adaptive
  corruption are out of scope.
- LLM-scale costs are our extrapolation from Theorem 6, not measurements.
- Niter tree passes per aggregation (each O(log n) rounds) add latency on wide-area links.
- Robust aggregation bounds damage; it does not detect backdoors. The median stays weak under heterogeneity
  and under ALIE-type attacks (Table III).
