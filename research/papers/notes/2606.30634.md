# 2606.30634 — One-Step Gradient Delay is Not a Barrier for Large-Scale Asynchronous Pipeline Parallel LLM Pretraining (Async PP with 2BW + Muon)

## 1. Citation

  Abdullaev (Yandex, BRAIn Lab, Innopolis University), Mikhail Khrushchev (Yandex), Samuel Horváth (MBZUAI).
  *Equal contribution.
- **Date and venue:** arXiv v1, 29 Jun 2026. ICML 2026 (PMLR 306), per the paper header.
- **Code:** no repository linked in the paper.

## 2. Summary
Asynchronous pipeline parallelism (Async PP) removes pipeline "bubbles" (idle slots while the pipeline fills and
drains) but trains on stale gradients. The PipeDream-2BW schedule updates once per full minibatch of M >= P-1
microbatches, so the staleness is exactly one step for any pipeline depth P. The paper argues that Async PP's
reputation for instability comes mostly from AdamW. Muon and several other modern optimizers barely degrade under a
one-step delay. An update-level error-feedback (EF) correction closes most of what remains. At scale, a 10B-parameter
mixture-of-experts (MoE) model trained on 200B tokens with Async PP plus EF matches synchronous validation loss with
identical hyperparameters.

## 3. Method
- **Delayed update (Alg. 1).** `x_{t+1} = x_t - u_{t-1}(g_{t-1})`: the gradient applied at step t was computed at
  `x_{t-1}`. 2BW stashes one extra weight version so each microbatch's forward and backward use the same weights.
- **EF correction (§3.2, Eq. 1).** `x_{t+1} = x_t - 2*u_{t-1}(g_{t-1}) + u_{t-2}(g_{t-2})`. This adds the gap between
  the update that should have been applied last step and the one that was.
  - It costs one extra parameter-sized buffer.
  - The same correction applied to raw gradients diverges (Fig. 19).
  - It equals SAPipe-WP weight prediction in displacement (App. B.1).
  - A scale lambda on the correction term shows a U-shaped effect, with the best value near 1 (App. A.4).
- **Hyperparameter findings (§2.2, Fig. 4, App. A.2).**
  - Higher momentum (beta1 or mu) consistently shrinks the gap between synchronous and asynchronous (sync-async)
    training. For Muon, mu = 0.99 beats 0.95.
  - Smaller batches shrink the gap; very large batches can push it above 0.1.
  - Delay amplifies existing instabilities, such as a high learning rate or short warmup.
- **Theory (§4, App. C).** Proves convergence of delayed Muon and other LMO (linear minimization oracle) methods,
  with and without EF, under smoothness and star-convexity. The delay adds a small noise term.
- **Setups (App. E).**
  - Dense: SmolLM2 135M and 360M, trained on FineWeb-Edu at 20 tokens per parameter with batches of about 180K and
    360K tokens.
  - 2B MoE (0.5B active; 64 experts, top-8 routing): 50-200B tokens.
  - 10B MoE (0.65B active): Qwen3-Next-like, with Gated DeltaNet linear-attention layers, 24 layers, and 512 experts
    with top-10 routing. 200B tokens, 4M-token batch, peak learning rate 0.00225, 64 GPUs.

## 4. Key reported results
- **Robustness to one-step delay (Table 1).** Validation loss going from synchronous to asynchronous at 360M:
  - Muon: 2.578 to 2.590 (+0.012).
  - Others: Adan +0.010, NorMuon +0.014, AdaMuon +0.017, SOAP +0.027, Lion +0.030, NAdam +0.043, MARS +0.282.
  - AdamW: 2.612 to 2.890 (+0.278).
  - At 135M: Muon +0.014 and AdamW +0.350.
- **EF at 360M (Table 2).**
  - Muon: 2.583 with EF, against 2.590 with no correction and 2.578 synchronous.
  - AdamW: 2.640 with EF, against 2.890 and 2.612.
  - EF recovers about 50-70% of the gap for Muon variants and SOAP, and 85-90% for AdamW and MARS. It slightly hurts
    Adan and NAdam.
  - Other fixes help less or not at all: a synchronous start, a synchronous cooldown, and DC-ASGD (delay-compensated
    asynchronous SGD) (§3.1, Table 9, Fig. 14).
- **Noise floor.** About 1e-3; the standard deviation for Muon at 135M is 3.9e-4 (App. A.7).
- **2B MoE.** The synchronous and asynchronous loss-vs-tokens curves stay parallel from 50B to 200B tokens, so the gap
  does not grow with training length (Fig. 5).
- **10B MoE at 200B tokens (Tables 3-4).** Synchronous, asynchronous, and asynchronous with EF:

  | Metric | Sync | Async | Async+EF |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Validation loss | 1.906 | 1.911 | 1.906 |
  | MMLU | 0.411 | 0.410 | 0.407 |
  | HellaSwag | 0.670 | 0.665 | 0.673 |
  | WinoGrande | 0.590 | 0.584 | 0.567 |

- **Variable vs constant delay (§6, Fig. 6, 135M).** Original PipeDream gives variable delays across stages.
  - At P = 4, the best Muon (mu 0.99) with EF reaches 2.840 against 2.841 synchronous.
  - At P = 16, the best configuration loses more than 0.03.
  - So constant delay (2BW) is what scales with depth.
- **Memory (App. F.1).** One stashed weight version plus one EF buffer added less than 1.5 GB per GPU in the 10B run.
- **Speed is modeled, not measured (App. F.2, Table 12).** At P = 16, a synchronous 1F1B schedule is 1.94x, 1.47x,
  and 1.23x slower than ideal Async PP for M = 16, 32, and 64 microbatches. Assuming backward costs twice forward
  (B = 2F), ZB1P is 1.31-1.08x slower and DualPipe 1.29-1.07x.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd
- **A clean contract for volunteer pipelines.** Flush and barrier semantics are unaffordable over a WAN, where
  pipeline fill, drain, and stragglers cost seconds. Here each stage computes on weight version v while v+1 is
  produced: one step stale at any depth. Quality held at 10B parameters and 200B tokens when the optimizer is from
  the Muon family, with EF.
- **Directly actionable for Agora-like swarms** (2607.13332). They use AdamW with a synchronous CPU optimizer step per
  stage. Switching to Muon with 2BW-style delayed updates should remove that stall without losing quality (untested
  there).
- **Deep pipelines are unavoidable on small cards.** A 27B model on 8-24 GB cards means tens of stages. The result
  that variable delay degrades by P = 16 argues against PipeDream-style immediate per-stage updates and for
  constant-delay schedules. FRESCO (2611.11238) and basis rotation (2602.03515) attack variable delay instead; both
  are runners-up.
- **Inner optimizer choice for asynchronous designs.** Covenant/SparseLoCo (2603.08163) and Decoupled DiLoCo
  (2604.21428) use AdamW inner loops. The evidence here that AdamW is fragile under staleness suggests Muon-family
  inner optimizers for any asynchronous design. This also matches ResBM's preference for Muon (2604.11947).
- **Trust: largely neutral.** The delayed update is deterministic given the gradients, so replay audits still work.
  A verifier must replay two steps of update history because of the EF buffer.

## 6. Caveats
- **Delay is simulated in the optimizer (Alg. 1).** There are no wall-clock measurements and no slow-link
  experiments. The throughput benefit comes only from a bubble model (App. F.2).
- **Constant delay needs strict conditions.** It requires M >= P-1 microbatches per minibatch and lockstep stage
  updates. A heterogeneous, churning swarm would see variable delays (not studied).
- **Limited dense evidence.** The largest dense model is 360M. The scaled runs are MoE models with only 0.5-0.65B
  active parameters, and the batch-size by learning-rate grid exists only at 135M.
- **Downstream results are mixed for Async+EF.** For example, WinoGrande is 0.567 against 0.590 synchronous
  (Table 4).
- **Interactions untested.** No heterogeneity, Byzantine peers, activation compression (ResBM, SSN), or
  DiLoCo-style outer optimization was tested.
