## 2. Summary
Agora trains a model that no participant can hold, on consumer-class GPUs joined only by the internet. The model is
cut into pipeline-parallel (PP) stages. Each stage is replicated over a changing set of GPU "workers" that hold only
that stage. GPU-less "trainers" route each microbatch through one worker per stage, as in SWARM parallelism.
Inter-stage activations and gradients are compressed about 100x by a reparameterized Subspace Network (SSN). Replicas
of a stage never exchange gradients: they step locally and stay in consensus by averaging a rotating 5% of weights
every 20 steps, in the background (AsyncSPARTA). Pluralis-8B (8.6B trainable parameters, 500B tokens, 40 days, 330
contributor nodes) sustained about 170k tokens/s, 63% of a centralized H100 baseline's tokens per pooled TFLOP.

## 3. Method
- **Workers (§3.3.1, Alg. 2).** Stateless: a worker recomputes its stage forward when the backward request arrives
  (stage-level activation checkpointing). Single-sample requests are coalesced into a batch sized to the worker's
  VRAM. AdamW runs on the CPU, with master weights and moments in host RAM.
- **Trainers (§3.3.2).** No GPU, no weights. A trainer holds the boundary activations, relays them head -> body ->
  tail and the gradients back, and picks workers with a speed-weighted min-heap. The backward pass is pinned to the
  forward's workers (§4.2.1), because replicas drift apart between averaging rounds.
- **PP compression (§4.1, Eq. 1-3).** The residual stream is confined to a shared k-dimensional basis `U_k`. Every
  attention/MLP output projection becomes `W = Z U_k^T` and the trainable embedding `T = E U_k^T`. A stage sends
  `(X - PE - T_fixed) U_k` (n x k values), and the receiver reconstructs exactly; the backward pass is compressed
  the same way. Settings: 100x for Pluralis-8B (rank about 51 at width d = 5120); rank 40 at d = 2048 (about 51x)
  in the 1B ablations.
- **Data-parallel (DP) axis (§4.2, Alg. 3).** AsyncSPARTA averages a rotating p = 5% slice every N = 20 steps, so
  every weight is averaged once per 400 steps. A butterfly all-reduce does this, with operator-only reducers. The
  "delta rule" (lambda = 1) adds `avg - snapshot` to the replica's already-advanced weights. A round may finish
  partially: on a timeout, a node keeps its local values.
- **No global barriers (§4.3).** Gradients are clipped per stage at 1/sqrt(S): 0.3779 for head and body stages,
  0.7558 for the tail. A stage steps when the sample count its workers publish to the stage's distributed hash
  table (DHT) reaches B = 2,048 sequences.
- **Joining (§3.3.1, Alg. 1).** A new node loads the stage checkpoint from cloud object storage. If it is more than
  3 steps stale, the node runs a weight-sync phase (400 steps at averaging weight 0, invisible to trainers), then an
  optimizer-sync phase (100 steps on real batches to warm the Adam moments, still at weight 0). A worker
  self-terminates after failing 2 of its last 3 all-reduce rounds.
- **Admission (§3.6.1, footnote 4).** A singleton Authorizer enforces minimum hardware (24 GB VRAM, 80 GB RAM,
  200 Mbit/s download, latency of 80 ms or less) and runs client-integrity checks (mechanism not described). It also
  tests reachability, rate-limits admission, assigns stages, and issues signed short-lived credentials that peers
  verify locally.
- **Pluralis-8B (Tables 1-2).**
  - Architecture: 32 blocks in 7 stages (head: 6 blocks + embeddings; 5 bodies of 4 blocks; tail: 6 blocks +
    LM head). d = 5120, grouped-query attention (GQA) 40/8, vocabulary 128,256, sequence 4,096.
  - Training: AdamW at learning rate 3e-4, betas (0.9, 0.95), weight decay 0.1, 4k warmup steps. Batch 2,048
    sequences (about 8.4M tokens).
  - Schedule: the learning-rate schedule length is 154,900 steps, but the run stopped at 60k steps.

## 4. Key reported results
- **The run (§5.1, Table 1).** 14 May to 23 Jun 2026, 500B FineWeb-Edu tokens, 30.8 ZFLOPs. Final training loss
  2.41 at 60k steps. A centralized TorchTitan reference with the same SSN and SPARTA changes ran 27k iterations and
  "sits slightly lower" (Fig. 19); the gap is not tabulated (unverified).
- **Fleet (§5.3, Figs. 14-16).**
  - 330 contributor nodes: RTX 4090 (91), RTX 5090 (90), L40S (74), RTX PRO 6000 (61), RTX 6000 Ada (14). They
    came from 176 operators (Acknowledgments). At most 60 at a time; 669 joins and 607 departures.
  - Median RAM 504 GB; median download 703 Mbit/s.
  - 3.33M join requests came from 612 IPs; 99.98% were rejected (89% because the run was full).
  - Topology (§5.4): head and tail on operator nodes only (8 and 16); each body stage had 4 operator nodes plus
    contributors, about 15 nodes per body stage at the median; 745 trainers.
- **Throughput (§5.4, Table 4, Fig. 17).**
  - About 170k tokens/s (peak 171.5k at 4.31 tokens/TFLOP over steps 20k-30k). Steady state 4.2 tokens/TFLOP
    (peak 5.45), 63% of a Llama-3.1-8B H100 FSDP2 baseline.
  - Model FLOPs utilization (MFU) 24.2% overall and 18.6-22.4% on bodies; body GPU utilization 42-50%.
  - Body p50 communication 128-150 ms against 255-306 ms of compute, so bodies were compute-bound.
- **Bandwidth used (Fig. 30b).** Body nodes averaged 85 Mbit/s of 714 Mbit/s available, with all-reduce bursts of
  157-165 Mbit/s. The worst-case estimated need is 113-186 Mbit/s (§3.5.2).
- **Latency, not bandwidth, sets node throughput (§5.10, Fig. 36).** Per-node throughput fell about 40% from the
  0-20 ms band to the 60-80 ms band (98 to 58 batches per interval). It was flat across 200-1000 Mbit/s, and only
  weakly tied to peak FLOPs (L40S 108 vs RTX PRO 6000 95 batches per interval). Latency and GPU class are confounded.
- **Coordination cost (§5.9).** After contributors joined, DHT get/set latency rose about 9-10x and body all-reduce
  time rose up to 8x. Contributor timeouts were frequent despite a 150 s timeout.
- **1B ablations (§6, Table 5).** Setup: d = 2048, 16 layers, 4 stages, 0.94B trainable parameters, L40S GPUs.
  - Reparameterized SSN = original SSN = central reference in loss (Fig. 38); the reparameterization makes body stages
    35% lighter and the head 50% lighter.
  - Unpinned (stochastic) backward routing ends 0.4-0.46 nats worse at step 8k (Fig. 41).
  - Heterogeneity up to about 10:1 tracks the homogeneous baseline (Figs. 42-43).
  - Convergence stalls while reducer participation is below about 85% and recovers once faulty nodes are pruned:
    loss went from 3.04 to 2.97 (Fig. 45).
- **Scaling argument (§3.4.5; analysis, not a measurement).** Per-stage compute over boundary traffic grows as
  L_s * d * c (layers per stage, width, compression rate). The DP all-reduce duty cycle does not change with scale.
  The usable rank grows slower than width.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd
- **The best existence proof so far for the owner's target.** Consumer-class GPUs pretrained a model that no single
  node held. This is the alternative to the full replica per peer that Covenant-72B / SparseLoCo (2603.08163) and
  Decoupled DiLoCo (2604.21428) assume.
- **Design lessons for 1B -> 27B -> millions of GPUs:**
  - **Latency per hop, not bandwidth, limits throughput.** Use fewer, deeper stages and latency-aware placement, and
    remove the backward round trip where possible (SOLO 2609.35440 forward-only boundaries would also cut trainer
    relays).
  - **Coordination failed first.** The per-stage DHT and all-reduce matchmaking degraded at only 60 contributors, so
    a million-GPU design needs hierarchical or sharded coordination.
  - **Async sparse weight averaging is a workable DP consensus.** It tolerated 10:1 heterogeneity and churn but
    needs at least about 85% participation, close to Decoupled DiLoCo's quorum idea. For elastic joining, stateless
    workers, checkpoints in object storage, and two sync phases are a concrete recipe.
- **Composes with** ResBM (2604.11947) as a simpler compressor; Muon with one-step-delayed asynchronous PP
  (2606.30634) to remove the synchronous CPU AdamW stall; MegaTrain-style weight streaming (2604.05091) so
  small-VRAM cards can host big stages; SparseLoCo on the DP axis (2601.02360 tested this; already known to us).
- **Trust is the weak point.** The run was permissioned in practice: operator-run head, tail, and reducers; a
  singleton Authorizer; integrity checks; signed credentials. The work itself is never verified. Reducers weight
  senders by self-reported sample counts, and stage steps fire on sample counts published to the DHT; both are
  spoofable. Body-stage activations and gradients are unchecked. SENTINEL (2603.03592) and Soft Throttling
  (2609.39357) each cover part of this; Crowd would need its own layer.
- **"No party holds the full weights"** is also an ownership lever (Unextractable Protocol Models: cited, not tested).

## 6. Caveats
- **Not decentralized in practice (§3.3).** The operator ran the head, the tail, and every reducer. Contributors were
  mostly rented datacenter-class machines (median 504 GB RAM, at least 24 GB VRAM and 200 Mbit/s), not 8 GB cards on
  20 Mbit/s home uplinks.
- **Small scale.** At most 60 concurrent contributors, about 15 nodes per stage, 7 stages. Hundreds of stages or
  thousands of replicas per stage are untested, and the DHT was already struggling.
- **Quality evidence is thin.** The learning-rate schedule was truncated (60k of 154.9k steps); there are no
  downstream evaluations; the reference run was shorter and the gap is read from a figure; the 1B ablations run only
  about 10k steps; and both runs use SSN, so its cost against a dense 8B model at 500B tokens is unmeasured.
- **Utilization.** MFU was 24% and the tail paced the pipeline. Partial all-reduces left visible inter-replica drift
  (Figs. 24-26).
- **No adversarial testing.** No Byzantine experiments; the "package verification" mechanism is not described
  (unverified).
