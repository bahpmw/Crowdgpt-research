## 2. Summary

Muon and SOAP are "matrix" optimizers: instead of rescaling each coordinate separately as AdamW does, they
precondition each 2D weight's update as a whole.

- **Muon** orthogonalizes the momentum with Newton–Schulz (NS) iterations: it keeps the singular vectors and
  sets every singular value to about 1.
- **SOAP** runs Adam inside the eigenbasis of Shampoo-style Kronecker factors.

Both beat AdamW in small studies, but stability, compute, and sharding have limited their use at scale. This
NVIDIA report tests them on an 8B dense model, 30B-total / 3B-active mixture-of-experts (MoE) models, and a
72B / 8B-active hybrid Mamba MoE, over 1–3T tokens, transferring learning rates (LR) fairly by matching update
RMS (root mean square). Muon and SOAP beat AdamW and keep quality at 2–4× the 25M-token baseline global batch
(up to about 100M tokens), while AdamW at 50M tokens does worse than at 25M. SOAP's large-batch instability
is fixed by a per-step eigenbasis refresh with the current gradient plus KL-Shampoo factors. A layer-wise
distributed optimizer makes both practical in Megatron-LM, and MXFP8 (8-bit microscaling floating point)
training with Muon holds up at 1T tokens.

## 3. Method

- **Muon** (Alg. 1, Table 4):
  - Momentum `M ← 0.9·M + 0.1·G`, no Nesterov (Sec. 5.2); normalize `M / max(‖M‖_F, 1e-7)`.
  - 16 NS iterations with PolarExpress coefficients.
  - Scale by `√max(in,out)` (Kimi update-RMS matching; matching factor ≈ 0.2 = `√((1−β1)/(1+β1))`).
  - Decoupled weight decay 0.1.
  - Applied only to dense 2D projections. AdamW keeps embeddings, norms, and Mamba conv1D filters, which do
    about 0.1% better in loss under AdamW (App. D, Fig. 12).
- **KL-SOAP** (Alg. 2, Table 6): Kronecker factors via KL-Shampoo (β_kron 0.95, exponent −1); eigenbasis
  recomputed by QR every step (F = 1) including the current gradient; Adam (β1 0.9, β2 0.95) in that basis.
- **Large-batch protocol** (Sec. 5.1–5.3, Table 3, Fig. 3):
  - Baseline global batch size (GBS) 3072 × 8192 ≈ 25M tokens, tested at 2×, 3×, and 4×.
  - Batch ramp-up from 256 sequences (2M tokens), doubling every S steps, with LR ∝ √batch (Eq. 3).
  - Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) schedule with minus-square-root decay; weight decay 0.1.
- **MoE batch argument** (Sec. 3.1, Eq. 2): each expert sees `B_global·k/N` tokens per step (e.g.,
  8/256 = 3.1%). Large global batches mainly stress the dense and shared parameters.
- **Systems** (Sec. 3.3, 6): under tensor parallelism, NS is "duplicated" (all-gather the weight) or
  "distributed" (all-reduce intermediates). Under data parallelism, whole matrices go to ranks round-robin by
  size; each rank updates its own layers and shares them by async allgather-V overlapped with the forward.
- **Models** (Tables 1–2): 8B dense GPT; Qwen3-30B-A3B (128 experts, top-8); Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B
  (128 experts, top-6); Nemotron-3-72B-A8B (512 experts, top-6, with multi-token-prediction, MTP, layers).
  Data: 1T/3T-token subsets of the Nemotron-3 dataset.

## 4. Key reported results

- **Baseline batch:** Muon reaches lower loss with fewer spikes than AdamW (Fig. 1). At 2×/3× batch it trails
  early, then overtakes over long horizons (Fig. 1 caption, 3T tokens).
- **Downstream, Nano-V3 30B-A3B at 3T tokens** (Table 5; Muon 1×/2×/3× vs AdamW 1×):
  - MMLU 73.71 / 74.80 / 74.00 vs 73.38; coding average 62.23 / 63.70 / 61.89 vs 62.22.
  - Math average 82.52 / 81.57 / 81.47 vs 79.65; commonsense 82.38 / 82.28 / 82.61 vs 81.89.
- **Downstream, 72B-A8B at 1T tokens** (Table 5; Muon 2× vs AdamW 1×): MMLU 74.89 vs 74.59; coding 63.48 vs
  58.97; commonsense 83.31 vs 82.66. Math 80.79 vs 84.04: AdamW wins here.
- **72B-A8B loss:** at 2× batch, Muon has lower loss than AdamW 1× and no spikes (Fig. 2). The largest runs
  are about 5e22 FLOPs (Sec. 5.3).
- **AdamW at larger batch** (App. C, Fig. 10): on Nano-V3 over 1T tokens, AdamW at 50M tokens, even with
  ramp-up and √ LR scaling, is worse than at 25M.
- **SOAP fixes:** a stale preconditioner causes "slingshot" spikes; a 621M model recovers (Fig. 4) but an 8B
  dense model diverges (Fig. 5). Per-step QR with the current gradient removes the spikes, and KL covariance
  estimation reduces them further (Fig. 6) while taking the square root of the condition number (Eq. 4).
- **Muon vs SOAP** (Qwen3-30B-A3B; Fig. 7 at 1×/2×/4× with no ramp-up; Fig. 8 at a fixed 24M batch):
  KL-SOAP has a slight edge, and so does MOP (Muon with an exact SVD polar factor). The authors recommend
  KL-SOAP when memory is not the limit (Sec. 7).
- **MXFP8 + Muon** (Table 7, 1T tokens, 2× GBS, BF16 vs MXFP8):
  - Nano: MMLU 69.33 vs 67.84; coding 56.55 vs 58.78; math 77.64 vs 76.31; commonsense 78.76 vs 79.46.
  - 8B-active LatentMoE: MMLU 74.89 vs 75.7; coding 63.48 vs 64.02; math 80.79 vs 83.15; commonsense
    83.31 vs 83.53.
  - Replacing AdamW with Lion (presumably on the non-Muon parameters) helped further (MMLU 76.00).
- **Not reported:** loss values in the text (figures only), layer-wise optimizer step time, measured memory.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **More volunteers per step.** A step's global batch grows with the number of contributors. Muon holding
  quality at 2–4× the batch where AdamW degrades raises how many volunteers can share one step, or one DiLoCo
  outer round, before tokens are wasted. But 100M tokens is only about 12k sequences of 8192 (our
  arithmetic). A million GPUs cannot feed one synchronous step, so local updates (DiLoCo, i.e. many local steps
  between syncs, or its Muon variant MuLoCo) or hierarchical aggregation are still needed. Muon raises the
  ceiling at each level.
- **MoE absorbs huge batches** (Eq. 2): experts see only k/N of the batch. A fine-grained MoE at the 27B
  stage lets the fleet grow mainly on expert parameters, with Muon on the dense and shared parameters that
  see the full batch.
- **Memory.** One momentum buffer instead of AdamW's two (Sec. 2) saves 4 bytes per parameter with FP32
  states (our arithmetic). It stacks with 4-bit Muon states (MuonQ, 2605.11396). Combining it with ECO
  (2601.22101) is untested: ECO's injection is derived for momentum SGD (SGDM) and Adam, and Muon is
  nonlinear in the momentum.
- **Low precision composes.** MXFP8 with Muon stayed close to BF16 at 1T tokens (Table 7). MXFP8 is a
  Blackwell format. Ada cards (RTX 4090/4060 Ti) have FP8 tensor cores but use per-tensor FP8 recipes (vendor
  fact, not from this paper; MXFP8 on Ada unverified).
- **Topology fit.** A full replica per volunteer already holds whole matrices, so no sharding machinery is
  needed. If Crowd later shards a model across volunteers, the "owner updates whole matrix" layer-wise design
  maps onto assigning matrices to workers.
- **Trust.** NS runs in BF16, leaving a noise floor of small singular values (Sec. 7). Replays will not be
  bit-identical across GPU types, so audits need tolerances. Update-RMS matching makes each matrix's step
  size predictable, which could serve as a cheap validator sanity check on inner updates (our idea,
  untested).
- **Round-1 links.**
  - Covenant-72B (2603.08163) uses AdamW inner steps (H=30). Muon would halve optimizer state and, per
    MuLoCo (2505.23725, not in round 1), give better pseudo-gradients as the worker count grows. This is
    untested in a permissionless setting.
  - Decoupled DiLoCo (2604.21428) learners could run Muon unchanged.
  - ZO-Muon (2602.17155) applies the same orthogonalization to zeroth-order estimates; this paper supplies
    tested numerics (16 PolarExpress iterations, ε = 1e-7).

## 6. Caveats

- Datacenter only: synchronous NVIDIA clusters on Megatron-LM. There are no low-bandwidth, local-update,
  asynchronous, or consumer-GPU tests, and no measured NS cost on small cards (unverified for a 4060 Ti).
- AdamW's β1, β2, and ε were not re-tuned for large batches (App. C), and Muon's and SOAP's ε were not
  tuned (Sec. 5.5). Part of AdamW's degradation may be a tuning artifact.
- Single runs with no variance reported; loss gaps appear only in figures (magnitudes unverified).
- Mostly hybrid Mamba-Transformer MoEs on NVIDIA data. Not every metric favors Muon (72B-A8B math).
