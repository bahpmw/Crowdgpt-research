# 2609.35440: SOLO, Pretraining Billion-Parameter Language Models with Shared-Output Local Learning

## 1. Citation

- **Title:** SOLO: Pretraining Billion-Parameter Language Models with Shared-Output Local Learning
- **Authors:** Bojian Yin (corresponding), Shurong Wang, Yuqi Pan, Guoqi Li. Institute of Automation,
  Chinese Academy of Sciences.
- **Date:** arXiv v1, 28 Sep 2026 (preprint)
- **arXiv:** 2609.35440 [cs.LG]

## 2. Summary

SOLO is a local-learning (gradient-isolated) method for transformer LM *pretraining*.

- **Split.** The network is cut into K consecutive modules. No gradient crosses a module boundary:
  each module's input is `stop_grad(h_{k-1})`.
- **Local loss.** Each non-final module trains on its own next-token CE loss through a small
  auxiliary head (H transformer blocks plus RMSNorm).
- **Shared readout, the one change from prior local learning.** Every auxiliary head predicts through
  a *read-only, stop-gradient copy of the terminal unembedding* `W`, scaled by a learned scalar
  temperature `tau_k`, instead of a private readout. Only the final module's loss updates `W`. Its
  previous-step copy therefore carries "what the top of the network wants" to every module, without
  passing gradients and without update locking.
- **Results.**
  - Quality: 340M-2B models trained on 15B SlimPajama tokens. With K=2, SOLO is within 0.5 points of
    backprop (BP) in average zero-shot accuracy and 3.1-3.6% in WikiText perplexity. The gap
    narrows with scale and does not grow during training.
  - Gradient alignment: local gradients have cosine 0.70 with BP, against 0.52 for private and 0.32
    for random readouts.
  - Systems: each pipeline stage holds O(1) micro-batches of activations instead of O(p). Stages
    send only activations forward, half the traffic of 1F1B. At a 1 Gb/s inter-stage link, SOLO loses
    1.2% of throughput while 1F1B loses 51%. Best throughput is up to 1.44x 1F1B.

## 3. Algorithm

**Notation:**

- K modules, `h_k = f_k(h_{k-1}; theta_k)`, `h_0 = x` (the token embedding is inside module 1).
- Terminal readout `W in R^{V x d}` and bias `b`.
- Auxiliary head for module k < K: `phi_k` (H Transformer++ blocks plus a final RMSNorm), a scalar
  temperature `tau_k`, and a bias `b_k`. Heads are discarded at inference, or kept as early exits.
- `sg(.)` is stop-gradient. `W_bar` is the read-only copy of W from the previous step, or refreshed
  every S steps.
- **Input embedding is NOT tied to W** (Appendix C).

**Algorithm 1 (one optimizer step):**
```
1: W_bar <- sg(W)                                   # copy from previous step (or every S steps)
2: h_0 <- x
3: for k = 1..K-1:
4:     h_k  <- f_k(sg(h_{k-1}); theta_k)
5:     l_k  <- CE( softmax( tau_k * W_bar @ phi_k(h_k) + b_k ), y )
6:     (theta_k, phi_k, tau_k, b_k) <- Step(grad l_k)  # module k done; free its activations
7: end
8: h_K <- f_K(sg(h_{K-1}); theta_K)
9: L   <- CE( softmax( W @ h_K + b ), y )
10: (theta_K, W, b) <- Step(grad L)                   # only L updates W
```
- In a single process, `W_bar = W.detach()` read before step 10 is exactly the previous-step copy, at
  no cost.
- In a pipeline, line 1 becomes a one-way broadcast of `W` (`(p-1)(Vd + V)` numbers) from the last
  stage every S steps.
- Gradients flow *through* `W_bar` into `phi_k` and `tau_k`, but never into W.

**Local gradient (Eq. 3):** `g_hat_k = J_k^T tau_k W_bar^T delta_k`, where `J_k = d phi_k(h_k)/d h_k`
and `delta_k = dl_k/dz_k = p_k - onehot(y)`.

**The BP gradient (Eq. 1):** `g_BP_k = M_k^T W^T delta`, where `M_k = dh_K/dh_k`.

**Gap decomposition (Eq. 4):**
```
g_hat_k - g_BP_k = J_k^T (tau_k W_k - W)^T delta_k   [readout]
                 + J_k^T W^T (delta_k - delta)       [residual]
                 + (J_k - M_k)^T W^T delta           [path]
```
SOLO zeroes the readout term up to `tau_k` and the staleness of `W_bar`. Prop. 1: the inner product
with BP is positive when `||delta_k - delta||_{G_k} < ||delta||_{G_k}`, i.e. when the head's
prediction is close to the final module's.

**Hyperparameters and defaults:**

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Recipe | Transformer++ (Yang et al. 2024), 24 layers, split into K = 2 or 4 equal modules. K = 8 rejected (+89% FLOPs at 340M with H=2). |
| Head depth | H = 2 by default. H=0 is a linear readout. H beyond 2 gives about 1 ppl per block at a cost of (K-1) blocks. |
| Refresh period | S = 1 in pretraining. S of 10-50 is harmless (within 0.4%); S=100 costs +1.9% ppl and S=200 +3.0%. The copy is harmless while it lags W by up to about 3% of its norm. |
| Temperature | `tau_k` learned per head. Initial value not stated. |
| Optimizer | Not restated; "Step" is the recipe optimizer (AdamW, bf16 in the systems runs), one per module. Effective batch 524,288 tokens in the systems sweeps. Data: 15B SlimPajama tokens, one pass, V = 32k. |
| Readout sweep | WikiText-103, 40M-512M parameters, K=4, two-block heads, copy refreshed every 100 steps. |
| FLOP model (Prop. 2) | Per token, block cost `b = 12d^2 + 2Td` MACs, readout cost `r = Vd`. `W_BP = 3Lb + 3r` and `W_SOLO = 3(L + (K-1)H) b + (2K+1) r` (an auxiliary readout costs 2r because it needs no weight gradient). With `gamma = r/(Lb)`: `rho = W_SOLO/W_BP`, `rho - 1 = ((K-1)H/L + 2gamma/3)/(1+gamma)`. Measured overhead: 11-13% FLOPs per token with K=2, H=2. |
| Pipeline speed | SOLO is faster than 1F1B when micro-batches per step `M < M* = (1+gamma)/(H/L + 2gamma/3)`, about L/H for small gamma. Example: M* = 69.9 for L=96, H=1. |

- **Relative perplexity gap** from 340M to 2B: 3.6% -> 3.1% (K=2) and 11.2% -> 6.1% (K=4). The gap

| Variant | 40M | 128M | 512M |
|---|---|---|---|

- **Table 9** (40M, H=0): SOLO 76.16. Rotated shared readout (ROT) 89.41. A single shared random
  matrix (RSHARE) 104.36 beats per-head random (RAND) 116.36. A *common basis* matters even without
  trained content.
- **Gradient cosine with BP** (40M): RAND 0.32, PRIV 0.52, SOLO 0.70, in the same order at every
  module.
- **Vision:** sharing gives no gain on CIFAR or ImageNet. The readout matters only when V/d is large
  (LM V/d = 26-85).
- **Pipeline** (96-layer 1.2B, 8x A100):
  - Activation peak at p=8 falls from 17.4 to 2.5 GB.
  - Throughput is 1.18x 1F1B at M=24 (S=50) and 1.44x 1F1B at the best micro-batch (Table 17: 72.9k

  - Traffic is half that of 1F1B.
  - Throttled to 1 Gb/s (Table 18): SOLO 58.9k vs 1F1B 28.1k tok/s.
  - **The 24-layer pretraining shapes are a counterexample:** SOLO runs at 0.72-0.91x 1F1B at a fixed
    micro-batch, because the heads are expensive relative to 3-12 blocks per stage.
- **Early exits:** at 1.3B with K=4, the 8-layer exit reaches 28.20 ppl, matching the full 340M BP
  model.

## 5. Classification for Crowd v1.5

**A replacement for DUST as the training signal,** or a hybrid architecture around it. Each consumer
GPU would hold and train *one module plus its head* with ordinary local backprop.

**DUST shortcomings addressed:**

- **Low gradient cosine and high variance.** Local backprop gradients reach about 0.7 cosine with
  full BP. That is far above ZO estimates. The quality loss is roughly 3-6% perplexity against BP.
- **Compute per useful step.** One forward plus one local backward, with about 11-13% FLOP overhead
  (K=2, H=2), against DUST's population of antithetic forwards.
- **Memory on consumer GPUs.** A node stores only its module's parameters, optimizer state, and one
  micro-batch of activations. A model K times larger than one GPU's capacity becomes trainable.
- **Communication and stragglers in model-parallel form.**
  - Only forward activations cross stages: `B*T*d` values per micro-batch. No backward traffic and no
    update locking.
  - The readout broadcast every S = 10-50 steps is tiny.
  - The method tolerates slow links: 1 Gb/s costs 1.2%.
  - Stages never wait on deeper stages, so a slow downstream peer does not stall upstream peers.
- **Not addressed:** Byzantine peers. A malicious upstream stage can feed poisoned activations, and a
  malicious final stage can corrupt the shared `W`. DUST's data-parallel averaging also still applies
  *within* each module's worker pool.

**Hybrid option.** Run DUST *inside* a module against the local loss `l_k`. This gives a shorter path
and a stronger readout signal, but does not reduce the perturbation dimension. Only worth testing if
local backprop is unavailable.

## 6. MVP recipe (2-10 min, M5 Max, MPS)

**Setup:** `research/lab/harness.py`, preset `10m` (6 layers, d=256, V=4096, seq 128, batch 8), on
real FineWeb-Edu tokens. **Use untied embeddings** (`tie_embeddings=False`) for SOLO *and* the BP
baseline. With tying, module 1's local loss would update `W` through `tok_emb`, which breaks "only L
updates W".

**Minimal pieces** (`research/lab/methods/solo.py`, about 120 lines):

1. **Split.** Partition `model.blocks` into K = 2 (3+3 layers) or K = 3 (2+2+2). Module 1 owns
   `tok_emb`. The final module owns `norm_f`, `head` (W), and a head bias (optional; our GPT has
   none, so drop `b`/`b_k` consistently).
2. **Heads.** For k < K: `phi_k = [Block] * H + RMSNorm`, with H = 1 (H = 2 if time allows).
   `tau_k = nn.Parameter(torch.ones(()))` (init 1 is our choice).
   Logits: `tau_k * F.linear(phi_k(h_k), model.head.weight.detach())`.
3. **Step.** Loop over modules. Run `h = f_k(h_prev.detach())`, compute the local CE, call
   `backward()`, step module k's own AdamW, and free the graph. Then run the final module with the
   true CE. Each module gets its own AdamW (lr 1e-3 to 3e-3, betas (0.9, 0.95), warmup 50, cosine;
   reuse the Adam baseline's settings).
4. **Refresh period S.** Keep `W_bar` as a buffer copied from `head.weight` every S steps
   (S in {1, 10, 50}).
5. **PRIV ablation.** The head has its own `nn.Linear(d, V)` with no sharing. This cheaply confirms
   the paper's central claim at 10M scale. Note the paper's V/d is 26-85, and ours is 4096/256 = 16,
   where Table 7's random-readout penalty is about 20%. So a smaller SOLO-vs-PRIV gap is expected.
6. **Cost accounting.**
   - `Cost(fwd=tokens * (1 + head_frac), bwd=tokens * (1 + head_frac))`, with `head_frac`
     approximately `(K-1)H/L` plus the readouts.
   - `up` = activations per boundary `B*T*d*2` bytes (bf16) if simulating a pipeline, or the
     module-gradient bytes if simulating data-parallel workers per module.
7. **Simulated crowd pipeline** (optional, +2 min):
   - Each module's input comes from a FIFO of upstream activations that is `lag` steps stale
     (lag in {0, 4, 16}). This mimics asynchronous stages on different peers.
   - Also inject an int8 quantization of the boundary activations.
   - Measure the degradation.

**Arms (each about 2 min, equal wall-clock and equal tokens):**

- Adam-BP (untied)
- SOLO K=2, S=1
- SOLO K=3, S=10
- PRIV K=2
- DUST (`methods/dust.py` defaults)

**Metrics:**

- Validation loss vs tokens and vs wall time.
- Per-module cosine between the local and the BP gradient on a fixed probe batch. Reuse
  `crowd_v15.dust.backprop_gradients` for the reference; parameter-space cosine per module, as in
  Table 9.
- Peak MPS memory per module, computed as if each module ran on its own GPU.

**Proves out if:**

1. SOLO K=2 reaches within about 5% of BP validation loss at matched tokens, and beats PRIV.
2. The per-module cosine is at least 0.5.
3. SOLO is at least 10x more token-efficient than DUST to reach DUST's final loss.
4. Activation staleness of 4 or more steps costs under 2% loss.

Points 3 and 4 decide whether it is a viable crowd architecture.

## 7. Caveats and unclear points

- **Not backprop-free.** Every module still needs a local backward pass and an optimizer. It removes
  *cross-module* gradients only. If the DUST goal is "no backward on clients" (for example, an
  inference-only runtime), SOLO does not fit.
- **The optimizer recipe is not restated** ("follows Yang et al. 2024"): LR, schedule, weight decay,
  and `tau_k` initialization are unknown. Training-token comparisons are matched, but SOLO uses
  11-31% more FLOPs per token.
- **Only K in {2, 4} was tested,** with at least 6 layers per module. Finer splits make the heads
  dominate: rho = 1.89 at K=8, H=2. For a crowd of many small GPUs, deep models with many blocks per
  stage are needed (Table 19: M* = 13.7-66.9 for 7B-70B shapes).
- **All systems results are single-node** (NVLink or tc-throttled loopback), on synthetic Zipf
  tokens. Cross-node and asynchronous refresh are listed as *future work*. Inter-stage traffic was
  computed from tensor shapes, not measured on the wire. Zero-bubble pipeline schedules were not
  compared.
- **Under data parallelism, BP is faster.** SOLO runs at 0.64-0.88x FSDP or DDP throughput on
  NVLink. Its advantage appears only off-RDMA links (2.2x over DDP on socket transport, Table 12) or
  under memory pressure.
- **Plain transformers only.** No MoE, grouped-query attention, long context, or post-training. No
  convergence guarantee. The paper studies synchronous stages; staleness of upstream activations and
  peer heterogeneity (the crowd case) are untested beyond the refresh period S of the readout copy.
- **The vocabulary-to-width ratio matters.** Sharing helps when V/d is large. Our 10M preset has
  V/d = 16, the low end of their sweep, so effects at MVP scale may understate the benefit.
