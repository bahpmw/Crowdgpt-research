# 2610.03457 — Cross-Facility LLM Pre-training on HPC: Elastic Aggregation, Data Leasing, and Queue-Aware Placement (Cross-Facility / DARL)

## 1. Citation

- **Title:** Cross-Facility LLM Pre-training on HPC: Elastic Aggregation, Data Leasing, and Queue-Aware Placement
- **Authors:** Zarè Palanciyan, Thomas van Osch, Douwe van der Wal, Tim Kok (SURF, Amsterdam); Olivera Kotevska
  (Oak Ridge National Laboratory)
- **Date:** arXiv v1, 2 Oct 2026 (cs.DC), 10 pages. No venue stated.
- **Code:** App. C says "The code released with this paper is Apache-2.0", but gives no URL (location unverified).
- **Built on:** DiLoCo (Distributed Low-Communication training), Flower 1.33 (federated learning), torchtitan.

## 2. Summary

Academic GPU time comes as separate allocations on different high-performance computing (HPC) systems. Each system
has its own accelerators, queue, walltime cap and network rules, and none share a filesystem. The authors pooled three
of them, up to 7,400 km apart, to pretrain one Qwen3-0.6B model: Snellius (NVIDIA H100, Amsterdam), LUMI and Frontier
(both AMD MI250X). The system has three parts:
1. An elastic DiLoCo-style outer step, for which zero, one or many live sites are all normal states.
2. DARL (Distributed Allocation and Remote Leasing), a data-leasing protocol with heartbeat-backed time-to-live (TTL)
   leases. Within an epoch, no sample is trained twice or lost, even through crashes, late joins and work stealing.
3. Queue-aware placement driven by unprivileged Slurm `sbatch --test-only` probes.

The headline is honest. At matched tokens and steps, cross-site training loses on quality: held-out perplexity (PPL)
34.7 against 28.2 for centralised training. Per-round overhead is flat at about 110 s, though. In a real 23.8 h run
with four site departures, no data was lost or duplicated.

## 3. Method

**Elastic aggregation (§3.1, Eq. 1).**
- Each site runs H inner AdamW steps (default 100) from the global model w(t), then uploads its delta Δ_i and its
  token count. The server applies a Nesterov step weighted by token share p_i: v(t+1) = w(t) + η·Σ p_i·Δ_i and
  w(t+1) = v(t+1) + β·(v(t+1) − v(t)), with η=0.7 and β=0.9. With β=0 and η=1 this is plain FedAvg.
- Three elasticity rules:
  1. A round with a single live contributor takes the full step (η=1).
  2. Each delta carries the merge round (generation) it started from. Stale generations are rejected, so "a requeued
     job that trained from an old global model cannot poison the average".
  3. The global step advances by the largest step count any contributor took.
- Transport: bf16 weights, 1.32 GiB per message. Messages go inline in gRPC (a remote-procedure-call framework) under
  its 2 GiB cap, otherwise they stream through a small HTTP PUT/GET blob store. An HTTP-only mode handles Frontier,
  whose forward proxy kills long-lived gRPC streams (Table 5).

**DARL (§3.2).**
- A single coordinator holds the only authoritative ledger; each block is unassigned, leased or committed.
- Every site holds the same pre-tokenised corpus, cut into M = ⌈N/K⌉ blocks of K samples (K = 1,024 windows, Table 3).
- A deterministic Fisher-Yates permutation from a shared seed s shuffles the blocks, so sites do not drift onto
  different topics. A digest of (N, K, s) is checked when a site registers.
- One lease = one inner phase: M_phase = max(1, ⌈H·B·W·A/K⌉) blocks. B is the per-rank batch, W the number of ranks and
  A the number of accumulation steps.
- TTL = α·(mean step time) + β·RTT, with α=2.5 and RTT the round-trip time. This β is unspecified and is not the
  Nesterov β. Heartbeats refresh the TTL. Expired leases return their untouched data to the pool.
- A site that has run out of data steals the untouched tail of the largest outstanding lease. Grant sizes follow each
  site's measured commit rate.
- Exactly-once rule: a site checkpoints *before* committing blocks, so its weights and its data record fail together.

**Placement (§3.3).**
- `sbatch --test-only` estimates the queue wait for each job shape. A discount from realised walltimes (`sacct`)
  corrects the estimate.
- A waterfill assigns data units to the soonest-available site in proportion to its throughput, and is recomputed
  every interval.
- A misprediction costs only calendar time, because a job that starts late leases nothing.

**Settings (Table 6).** Qwen3-0.6B, 5B tokens of C4, sequence length 2048, bf16, peak learning rate 3e-4,
micro-batch 8, global batch 96 (or 32 on Snellius + 64 on LUMI/Frontier).

## 4. Key reported results

- **Fleet (Table 5).**
  - Snellius: 4×H100 per node, InfiniBand, CUDA, direct egress, 120 h walltime cap.
  - LUMI: 8 MI250X GCDs (graphics compute dies) per node, ROCm in Apptainer, NAT egress, 48 h.
  - Frontier: same hardware as LUMI, HTTP(S) proxy, 24 h, full nodes only.
  - RTT to the coordinator: 3, 70 and 347 ms respectively (Table 3).
- **Quality (§4, Fig. 1).**
  - Centralised training reached PPL 28.2 in 5 h 41 min; two-site DiLoCo reached 37.5 in 19 h 48 min. The three-site
    elastic run reached 34.7 (abstract).
  - Throughput: 55k tok/s for two-site DiLoCo against 192k tok/s centralised.
  - A higher peak learning rate alone moves centralised training from 28.2 to 24.5.
- **Overhead vs H (Table 1, LUMI only).**
  - H=100: 235.9 s compute + 111.9 s overhead (32.2%).
  - H=1,000: 1,774.2 + 107.0 s (5.7%).
  - H=2,000: 3,601.8 + 115.8 s (3.1%).
  - Communication alone took 94.5, 90.4 and 98.7 s. At H=1,000 a 1,881 s round is training 1,711 s, evaluation 65 s,
    merge 17 s and communication 90 s (Fig. 2b).
- **Heterogeneity (Fig. 2a).** With equal batches the fast site idled 127 s (75% of the phase). Calibrating gradient
  accumulation (a_Snellius=5) brought the sites within 13 s of each other (8% idle).
- **Transport (Table 2).** Blob/HTTP overhead was 161.8 s against 176.0 s for inline gRPC (−8.1%). gRPC sent 310
  separate per-tensor fields.
- **Data leasing (Table 3).**
  - Ledger totals: 2,692 blocks; 326 grants; 2,070 blocks granted; 2,045 committed; 25 reclaimed by TTL (1.2%) over
    4 expiries; zero duplicates.
  - The 5.5 : 3.0 : 1.0 split (Frontier : LUMI : Snellius) emerged from commit rates alone, with no configuration.
  - Without leasing, identical loaders would all walk the same prefix: 1,181 unique blocks instead of 2,045 (1.73× less
    for the same compute).
  - Frontier's first job died holding 9 blocks. They were re-granted (5 to LUMI, 4 to Frontier's restart) and each was
    committed exactly once.
- **Scheduler (Table 4; idealised, no barrier waits).** A 100B-token run on LUMI + Snellius takes:
  - 1.11 days with no queue;
  - 2.28 days if Snellius is busy for 40 h;
  - 1.58 days if LUMI is busy for 40 h, because Snellius finishes alone in 37.8 h.
  The abstract projects 18–43% shorter time-to-target than waiting for all sites.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **Ready-made orchestrator part.** DARL is the "who trains which tokens" ledger that a churning volunteer fleet needs:
  TTL leases, work stealing, grants sized to measured speed, and exactly-once ordering. In Crowd the commit point should
  be the orchestrator *accepting* a validated submission, and rejected or timed-out work should go back to the pool.
  This adaptation is ours and is untested.
- **Size of the failure unit.** A departure forfeits one lease, i.e. one inner phase of data plus its compute (§4). H
  should make a phase much shorter than a typical volunteer session; session lengths are unverified.
- **Many orchestrators.** The deterministic permutation plus the (N,K,s) digest make it easy to give each independent
  orchestrator a disjoint block range. That would remove the paper's single coordinator VM as a single point of
  failure (inference, untested).
- **Staleness is a design choice Crowd must make.**
  - This paper rejects stale generations: simple, but slow volunteers' work is wasted.
  - Decoupled DiLoCo (2604.21428) accepts late learners through a quorum K and token-weighted merging.
  - Covenant-72B (2603.08163) uses fixed 20-minute windows.
  - At volunteer churn rates, rejection could waste a large share of compute (unquantified).
- **Cheapest heterogeneity fix.** For synchronous rounds, size each participant's batch or accumulation to its speed:
  idle time fell from 75% to 8%.
- **Mixed vendors work for DiLoCo merging.** CUDA H100s and ROCm MI250Xs trained one model by exchanging bf16 weights.
  Bitwise cross-vendor reproducibility, which exact replay audits like Crowd v1.5's need, was not tested.
- **Home networks.** The most locked-down site allowed only short HTTP requests through a proxy, much like home NAT and
  firewalls. Plain HTTP(S) to a blob store was also faster. This matches Covenant's object-storage design.
- **Bandwidth check (our arithmetic, not the paper's).** The paper sent dense bf16 weights over datacenter links in
  about 90–99 s. A 1B model in bf16 (about 2 GB) would need about 160 s at 100 Mbit/s and about 800 s at 20 Mbit/s
  upload, so Crowd still needs SparseLoCo-style compression (Covenant).
- **Sober quality point.** At 0.6B with few sites and matched steps, DiLoCo lost to centralised training. The authors
  attribute Photon's "federated beats centralised" result to roughly 8× more optimiser steps.

## 6. Caveats

- Proof of concept only: one 0.6B model, one epoch (5B tokens), three sites, no downstream benchmarks (§5, the
  authors' stated limitations).
- Trusted datacenter sites with fast links. There is no adversary model and no validation of deltas beyond the
  generation check.
- The single coordinator VM is a single point of failure, and ledger throughput at thousands of participants is
  untested.
- The 18–43% scheduler gain is a projection, not a measurement. Overhead numbers come from LUMI only.
- No compression, and no tolerance of stale updates.
