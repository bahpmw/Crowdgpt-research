# 2610.19064 — How Inner Optimizers Shape Replica Scaling (Replica scaling)

## 1. Citation
- **Title:** How Inner Optimizers Shape Replica Scaling
- **Authors:** Eugene Belilovsky (Mila, Concordia University), Stefan Horoi (Mila, Université de Montréal),
  Edouard Oyallon (CNRS), Benjamin Thérien (Mila, Université de Montréal)
- **Date:** arXiv v1, 13 Oct 2026 (cs.LG), preprint. **Venue:** none stated. **Code:** none linked.
- **Context:** DiLoCo scaling laws (Charles et al., 2503.09799), MuLoCo (Muon as the inner optimizer) and IsoLoCo
  (2607.03011, already known to us). SPICE (2611.23053) adopts this paper's β2 rule.

## 2. Summary
DiLoCo (Distributed Low-Communication training) is the base method: each replica takes many local optimizer
steps, then the replicas average their "pseudo-gradients" (parameter deltas). At a fixed token budget and a fixed
global batch, adding DiLoCo replicas R makes the final loss worse than data-parallel (DP) training. This is the
"replica scaling" problem.

The paper argues that the main cause is not multi-step drift. It is the adaptive inner optimizer's **nonlinear
transform applied before averaging**, and this shows up even with one local step (H = 1). The error against the
DP update splits into a *mean mismatch* and a *stochastic error* (Prop. 1).
- **Stochastic error.** "Whitening" inner optimizers make the update covariance isotropic (equal in every
  direction). That minimizes the worst-direction noise and makes plain uniform averaging optimal. SOAP is Adam run
  in Shampoo's eigenbasis; SPlus takes the sign of the momentum in that basis. Both whiten; AdamW does not.
- **Mean mismatch.** For Adam, the mismatch bound grows linearly with R at fixed β2 (Prop. 3). The fix is to
  scale β2 so that its memory, counted in local tokens, stays constant (Eq. 20).

Empirically, SPlus loses only 2.1% against its own DP at R = 96 (512M), while AdamW loses 12%.

## 3. Method
- **Framework (Algorithm 1):** DiLoCo with R replicas, H = 30 inner steps, local batch B/R, a uniform average of
  pseudo-gradients, and an outer Nesterov SGD. Inner optimizer states are never communicated.
- **Optimizers compared (Sec. 4.1):** AdamW; Shampoo; SOAP; an SPlus variant
  (`U = Q_L sign(Q_Lᵀ M Q_R) Q_Rᵀ`, Eq. 6, no second moment, no iterate averaging); Muon (polar factor of the
  momentum).
- **Theory.** Eq. 12: isotropy minimizes the largest directional variance at a fixed total variance. Sec. 4.3
  compares how each optimizer reshapes update noise:
  - Adam normalizes each coordinate but keeps correlations.
  - Canonical Shampoo only square-roots the condition number.
  - SOAP whitens if its basis decorrelates and the variances are known.
  - SPlus whitens without estimating variances and shrinks residual correlations (arcsine law, Eq. 13).
  - Muon is isotropic only under strong symmetry assumptions.
- **Props.:** Prop. 2: SPlus with a √R scale matches the DP mean to third order. Prop. 3: Adam's one-step
  mean-mismatch bound is proportional to (1 − β2)(R − 1). Rule (Eq. 20): `β2(R) = β2(R0)^(R0/R)`.
- **Setup (Sec. 5, App. B, Table 7):**
  - LLaMA models: 178M (9 layers, width 1024) and 512M (12 layers, width 1536), DCLM data, sequence length 2048.
  - 178M: 524K tokens per inner step (256 sequences), 3.55B tokens. 512M: 2.1M tokens per step (1024
    sequences), 10.25B tokens.
  - The global batch is fixed, so the local batch shrinks with R (4 sequences per replica at R = 64, 178M;
    App. C.1).
  - Hyperparameters (HP) swept at R = 8/64/96 and interpolated for R = 16/32 (App. B). Every second-moment
    optimizer uses the β2 rule in Sec. 5.1.
  - Absolute β2 values and the SOAP/SPlus basis-refresh frequency are not reported (we did not find them).

## 4. Key reported results
- **178M validation loss, DP → R = 64 (Table 1):**
  - SOAP 2.816 → 2.951 (+4.8%); SPlus 2.853 → 2.942 (+3.1%).
  - Muon 2.858 → 3.099 (+8.5%); AdamW 2.880 → 3.208 (+11%); Shampoo 2.842 → 3.069.
  - SPlus at R = 8 (2.851) slightly beats its own DP (Sec. 5.1).
- **512M, DP → R = 96 (Table 2):**
  - SOAP 2.563 → 2.676 (+4.4%); SPlus 2.610 → 2.663 (+2.1%).
  - Muon 2.586 → 2.787 (+7.7%); AdamW 2.646 → 2.958 (+12%).
  - SOAP has the best absolute loss through R = 32; SPlus is best at R ≥ 64.
- **H = 1 control (Table 3; 178M, plain parameter averaging every step), DP → R = 64:** AdamW 2.880 → 3.157,
  Muon 2.858 → 3.417 (worse than AdamW), SPlus 2.853 → 2.912, SOAP 2.816 → 2.958. Replica-scaling loss appears
  without any multi-step drift.
- **β2 rule (Fig. 4–5; 512M, R = 64), changing only β2:** DiLoCo 2.884 → 2.849, IsoLoCo 2.734 → 2.702 (about 1.2%
  each). With the scaled β2, IsoLoCo beats MuLoCo (reference runs: Muon 2.738, SOAP 2.649, SPlus 2.646).
- **Why it happens (178M checkpoints):**
  - Update anisotropy at R = 64 after one step (a1, lower is more isotropic; Table 4): AdamW 35, Muon 3.8,
    SOAP 2.3, SPlus 0.43. After 30 steps (a30): 11 / 3.3 / 2.5 / 2.4.
  - Mean mismatch is only 7.5–19% of the stochastic error (Table 5).
  - Loss curvature along the noisiest direction at R = 64, relative to random directions (Table 6): AdamW
    460–730×, Muon 47–100×, SOAP 4.2–5.2×, SPlus 1.5×.
- **Authors' claim (Sec. 5.2):** 50+ replicas with < 3% degradation using SOAP or SPlus. SOAP exceeds 3% at R = 64
  in both tables (4.8% at 178M; about 3.4% at 512M by our arithmetic), so only SPlus fits the claim throughout.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd
- **Replica count is the axis that explodes at million-GPU scale.** The paper gives two cheap, local levers, and
  neither changes anything on the wire, so they compose with SparseLoCo Top-k + error feedback, SPICE/GASLoC
  gossip and Decoupled DiLoCo quorums:
  1. Scale AdamW β2 with the local batch b. Our restatement of Eq. 20 is β2(b) = β2_ref^(b/b_ref), so β2 rises
     toward 1 as the local batch shrinks. SPICE used 0.95 → 0.997 for R = 8 → 128.
  2. Use a whitening inner optimizer (SPlus/SOAP) when local batches are small, which is exactly what 8–24 GB
     consumer cards impose.
- **Aggregation and trust.** Uniform averaging is near-optimal when replica covariances match (Sec. 4.2). Crowd
  then does not need weights from self-reported token counts, which a liar can inflate (as with Decoupled
  DiLoCo's token weights). SPlus updates have a fixed size in the rotated basis (signs times the learning rate),
  so honest update norms are predictable and norm screening (as in Covenant's median-norm rescaling) gets sharper.
  This last point is our hypothesis, not tested.
- **Memory cost on consumer GPUs.** The paper says SOAP/SPlus use more memory than Muon but gives no numbers. Our
  arithmetic: a d×d matrix with two-sided factors and eigenbases plus momentum needs about 5d² fp32 values,
  against 2d² for AdamW. That is significant for 1B+ on 8–24 GB cards; blocked or one-sided variants would be
  needed (unverified). IsoLoCo plus scaled β2 is the memory-light option, because its SVD runs only at the outer
  step.
- **Million-GPU implication (our inference).** At a fixed token budget, 10^6 replicas leave each replica only a
  few hundred local steps. A large network should not mean a large R per model: group GPUs into fewer, bigger
  replicas (LAN islands or pipelines, buckets B2/B7), overtrain, or train several models or experts (bucket B3,
  SOMA-like). This paper quantifies how fast quality drops as R rises.
- **Relation to round 1.** Covenant-72B (2603.08163) used AdamW inner with ≤ 20 contributors; Decoupled DiLoCo
  (2604.21428) used M = 8–16 learners. Neither tested R ≥ 64, and this paper predicts that AdamW with a fixed β2
  degrades badly there. Zeroth-order lines (DUST, SOMA 2609.37899) have no adaptive inner optimizer, but the
  "transform before averaging" insight warns against normalizing each client's estimate before averaging.

## 6. Caveats
- **Fixed global batch**, so the local batch shrinks with R. In a volunteer network the global batch grows with R
  instead (each GPU keeps its batch). That regime and the critical-batch-size limit are not tested.
- **Small and narrow.** Models of 178M and 512M at about 20 tokens per parameter; R ≤ 64 (178M) and ≤ 96 (512M);
  H = 30 plus an H = 1 control; one dataset (DCLM).
- **Statistics and tuning.** No seeds or error bars reported (we did not find any). HPs for R = 16/32 are
  interpolated, not tuned.
- **Theory.** One step, Gaussian, fixed-basis assumptions (Props. 2–3). Whitening single steps does not whiten
  the 30-step pseudo-gradient (Sec. 4.3, "Beyond one local step").
- **Not measured.** No wall-clock, memory or communication figures; no Byzantine, churn, heterogeneity or non-IID
  tests.
- **SPlus variant.** It differs from Frans et al. (no iterate averaging); its β values and refresh schedule are
  not given.
