# 2610.24343 — What Matters for NVFP4 Training? A Comparative Study of Low-Precision Pretraining Recipes (Mosaic NVFP4)

## 1. Citation

- **Title:** What Matters for NVFP4 Training? A Comparative Study of Low-Precision Pretraining Recipes
- **Authors:** Anjulie Agrusa, Andrei Panferov, Elizabeth Wei, Keith Wyss, Paul Gibbons, Erik Schultheis,
  Jinhang Choi, Frank Sun, Tijmen Blankevoort, Dan Alistarh
- **Affiliations:** NVIDIA; Institute of Science and Technology Austria (ISTA); Red Hat AI (Alistarh)
- **Date:** arXiv v1, 17 Oct 2026 (cs.CL). 10 pages including appendix.
- **Venue:** ICML 2026 Workshop on Resource-Adaptive Foundation Model Inference (AdaptFM). Workshop paper.
- **Code:** none linked in the paper. The main new ingredient, MS-EDEN, comes from Quartet II
  (arXiv 2601.22813, ICML 2026), which does release code (github.com/IST-DASLab/Quartet-II).

## 2. Summary

NVFP4 is NVIDIA's 4-bit floating-point (FP4) block format: small blocks of values share a higher-precision
scale. Blackwell GPUs run NVFP4 matrix multiplies on their tensor cores. Many published FP4 training recipes
were only tested on small models over short runs, and they disagree about which tricks are needed. This
paper puts the ingredients of existing recipes into one training stack. It stacks them one at a time on two
NVIDIA hybrid Mamba-Transformer models trained for up to 1T tokens: a dense 8B model and a 30B-total /
3B-active mixture-of-experts (MoE) model. The combined recipe, "Mosaic NVFP4", ends within about 1.2% (dense)
and 0.81% (MoE) relative training loss of BF16 (bfloat16) training. Three lessons matter. Keeping the last 15%
of layers in BF16 is still required: with every layer in FP4, the 8B model diverges after 300B tokens.
De-biasing appears to matter most on the gradient tensors. Recipe rankings only settle after more than 150B
tokens, so short ablations can mislead.

## 3. Method

- **The three GEMMs (general matrix multiplies) of a linear layer** (Sec. 3): forward `Y = X·W`;
  DGRAD `G·Wᵀ` (gradient with respect to the input); WGRAD `Gᵀ·Xᵀ` (gradient with respect to the weight).
  When both operands of a GEMM are NVFP4 along the contraction dimension, Blackwell FP4 tensor cores can run it.
- **Baseline "NVIDIA '25 NVFP4"** (arXiv 2509.25149): 2D (16×16) weight quantization so the forward and
  backward passes share one weight representation; stochastic rounding (SR) on gradients; a static 16-wide
  random Hadamard transform (RHT, a random rotation that spreads outliers) on WGRAD; some layers kept in BF16.
- **Stacked updates** (Table 1, Sec. 4.1):
  1. 1D weight quantization. In the backward pass, re-quantize the dequantized forward weights along the
     backward contraction dimension.
  2. 128-wide tiled RHT on both WGRAD and DGRAD, with random sign vectors resampled during training.
  3. MS-EDEN on the gradient tensors (G, Gᵀ) in place of SR. MS-EDEN is a random rotation plus a per-block
     corrective rescale, giving an unbiased estimate with lower error than SR (from Quartet II). Four Over Six
     (4/6) is added to the other backward tensors (Wᵀ, Xᵀ). 4/6 adaptively chooses between two scale factors
     per block (Sec. 2), which lowers error on near-maximum values (2512.02010 abstract).
  4. 4/6 is also applied to the forward tensors (W, X). The full stack is **Mosaic NVFP4**.
  All recipes keep the last 15% of layers in BF16 unless stated otherwise.
- **Training setup** (App. A.1, Tables 2–3): AdamW; Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) learning-rate (LR) schedule
  (anneal over the last 20% MoE / 15% 8B); weight decay 0.1; sequence length 8192.
  - **Nemotron-H 8B (dense):** 24 Mamba-2, 4 attention, and 24 feed-forward (FFN) layers; batch 768 sequences
    (≈6.3M tokens); LR 8e-4 → 8e-6.
  - **Nemotron 3 Nano (MoE):** 23 Mamba-2, 6 attention, 23 MoE layers; 128 routed experts (top-6) plus 2
    shared; 3.2B active / 31.6B total; batch 3072 sequences (≈25M tokens); LR 1e-3 → 1e-5.
  - **Data:** the Nemotron-H blend (600B diverse, then high-quality) / the first 1T tokens of Nano Phase 1.
- **Extra randomness** (Sec. 4.1): resampling the SR pattern of corrected scales, and drawing DGRAD RHT signs
  independently for each data-parallel replica, gave a further 0.028 percentage points (pp) on the MoE model
  (different optimizer config). The main results do not use this.

## 4. Key reported results

- **Stacking** (Fig. 1 right, MoE model to 400B tokens): every update lowers training loss, for 0.36 pp total.
  The largest single gain comes from update 3 (MS-EDEN plus 4/6 on the backward tensors).
- **1T tokens** (Sec. 4.4; Figs. 1, 4): 8B dense: Mosaic ends below 1.2% relative training-loss gap to BF16,
  0.24 pp better than NVIDIA '25. 30B-A3B MoE: below 0.81%, 0.36 pp better. Both gaps grow during annealing.
- **Noise floor** (App. A.2.1, three repeats of the NVIDIA '25 recipe): run-to-run loss spread at 1T tokens
  is 0.06% train / 0.04% validation for the MoE model and 0.027% / 0.064% for the 8B model. The recipe
  differences exceed this spread.
- **Full FP4 fails late** (Fig. 2, Sec. 4.2): Mosaic with every layer in NVFP4 diverges on the 8B model
  after 300B tokens.
- **Backward ablation** (Fig. 3, Sec. 4.3): MS-EDEN on all backward tensors is no better than MS-EDEN on
  gradients only. With all layers in FP4, MS-EDEN on G/Gᵀ plus 4/6 on Xᵀ/Wᵀ gives the lowest gap, but the
  margin is small. With the BF16 tail, the variants are indistinguishable.
- **Rankings flip** (Sec. 5): the order of recipes does not stabilize until well past 150B tokens, and weaker
  recipes often look better early.
- **Downstream** (Table 4): compares Mosaic against NVIDIA '25 only; there is no BF16 row.
  - Two changes fall outside the three-run noise reference: MMLU on the MoE model (67.5 → 68.4, +1.3%,
    noise 0.9%) and RACE on the 8B model (77.7 → 78.8, +1.4%, noise 1.1%).
  - Everything else is within noise. Example: 8B MMLU-Pro 29.7 → 27.6, noise 9.8%.
- **Not reported:** throughput, memory, wall-clock, and the exact GPU model used. The paper says only
  "the same GPU architecture" (Sec. 3).

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **Closest evidence at roadmap scale.** 8B dense and 30B-A3B MoE over 1T tokens bracket Crowd's 9B and
  ~27B-MoE stages. FP4 matrix multiplies can stay within about 1% loss of BF16 there, if a BF16 tail of about
  15% of layers is kept. The architecture is a hybrid Mamba-Transformer, not Crowd's GPT, so this does not
  transfer directly.
- **Compute lever, not memory lever.** The recipe quantizes GEMM operands only. Master weights, optimizer
  states, and the BF16 tail stay in high precision, and the paper reports no memory savings. On its own it
  does not ease Crowd's per-card memory limit. Pair it with master-weight removal (ECO, 2601.22101) and
  low-bit optimizer states.
- **Hardware split.** Only Blackwell cards get the FP4 speedup: in Crowd's fleet the RTX 5090 (other RTX 50
  cards share the architecture; unverified). Quartet II ships RTX 5090 and B200 kernels with "up to 4.2×"
  speedup over BF16 (2601.22813 abstract/HTML, not this paper; which GPU reaches 4.2× not checked).
  RTX 3090/4090/4060 Ti and Apple silicon have no FP4 tensor cores (vendor specs, not from this paper), so
  they would emulate FP4 at no speedup or run another precision. A mixed fleet must choose one recipe for all
  (losing the speedup) or per-class recipes whose updates come from slightly different numerics.
- **Trust and verification cost.** The recipe is randomized: SR, resampled RHT sign vectors, and MS-EDEN
  rotations. The authors even gain from per-replica independent signs. Crowd's audit (DEEPDIVE.md) only
  works where the estimate is reproducible. Every random draw would have to come from a public seed (round,
  worker, step, tensor). Bit-exact replay across GPU generations, or between FP4 hardware and emulation, should
  not be assumed; no paper here tests it. Audits would need to be device-class-matched or tolerance-based.
  The measured 0.03–0.06% run-to-run loss spread gives a first scale for how much noise an honest worker
  produces at the loss level.
- **Method lesson for the lab.** Rankings that flip before 150B tokens, and divergence that appears after 300B
  tokens, mean Crowd's small lab runs cannot validate a low-precision recipe. Adopt a published recipe as-is,
  BF16 tail included. During real runs, track the gap against a small BF16 "canary" replica.
- **Composes with round 1.** Covenant-72B (2603.08163) peers are 8×B200 nodes, so FP4 inner steps would
  run natively there. SparseLoCo's 2-bit, Top-k pseudo-gradient compression is independent of inner-loop
  precision. Decoupled DiLoCo (2604.21428) learners could form precision classes; its quorum and token-weighted
  merge already tolerate unequal learners. SOLO (2609.35440) modules could each use FP4, since its savings
  (activations, pipeline traffic) are separate from GEMM precision.

## 6. Caveats

- A 10-page workshop paper, with results mostly in figures. The exact loss curves cannot be read from the text
  (unverified beyond the quoted bounds).
- Two NVIDIA hybrid Mamba models with NVIDIA data. Untested on pure Transformers, other datasets, beyond 1T
  tokens, or above 8B dense / 30B-A3B MoE. The authors themselves name larger runs as future work (Sec. 6).
- Datacenter Blackwell and a synchronous stack are implied. There are no consumer GPUs, no
  heterogeneity, and no low-bandwidth or local-update (DiLoCo-style) training.
- Downstream results compare two FP4 recipes against each other, not against BF16.
- Optimizer is AdamW only. Interaction with Muon or ECO is untested.
