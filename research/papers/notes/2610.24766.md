# 2610.24766 — ECHO: Efficient Causal Self-Recursion Beyond Auxiliary Multi-Token Prediction (ECHO)

## 1. Citation

- **Title:** ECHO: Efficient Causal Self-Recursion Beyond Auxiliary Multi-Token Prediction
- **Authors:** Yuxuan Zhou (project lead), Junyuan Shang, Tianyu Wang, Chunrui Zheng, Zichang Liu, Wenbin Chang,
  Shuohuan Wang, Dianhai Yu. ERNIE Team, Baidu, plus one independent researcher.
- **Date:** arXiv v1, 17 Oct 2026 (cs.CL). No venue stated.
- **Code:** https://github.com/ernie-research/ECHO (title page; we did not check the repo).

## 2. Summary

Causal multi-token prediction (MTP) in the DeepSeek-V3 style attaches a separate module per extra prediction
depth. Each depth costs another module (parameters) and another pass (compute), and most of the extra
supervision trains that module rather than the backbone. That is why production models stop at depth 1. **ECHO**
predicts every future horizon by re-applying the backbone's own final decoder layer and output head recursively.
A small fusion layer joins the previous hidden state with the embedding of the next token. **Dynamic Depth
Sampling (DDS)** draws one prediction depth per optimizer step from a decaying distribution. This halves the MTP
training overhead while still giving occasional deep supervision. On a 10B-total / 1B-active mixture-of-experts
(MoE) model trained on 600B tokens, ECHO beats DeepSeek MTP on reasoning and code at equal or half the MTP cost.
The trained model is also its own speculative-decoding drafter and keeps gaining when it drafts beyond its trained
depth.

## 3. Method

- **Self-recursion (Eq. 1):** for t > 1, h_t = T_L(F(h_{t−1}, e(x_t))), where T_L is the backbone's last decoder
  layer and F is the fusion layer (RMSNorms, concatenation, linear projection; Fig. 2). Apart from F, every
  parameter used for future prediction also generates the next token. The same layer can therefore be unrolled
  past the trained depth at inference.
- **DDS (Sec 3.2, Eq. 2 and 4):**
  - Each step draws D ~ π over {1..K} and unrolls depths 1..D.
  - Overhead is E[D] "depth-1 units": unrolls of the shared layer on top of the backbone, not end-to-end cost.
  - Default π = (0.6, 0.3, 0.1), K = 3, so E[D] = 1.5. Depth k is trained on a fraction f_k = P(D ≥ k) of steps,
    giving f = (1, 0.4, 0.1).
  - The loss is averaged over the depths trained that step. All micro-batches in a step share one D, which keeps
    per-step compute constant.
  - D is a deterministic function of (seed, step), so ranks agree without communication and resumed runs replay
    the same sequence (App. A.1).
- **Theory (Sec 4.1, Eq. 5, App. B):** a lower bound on expected accepted length (union bound, then Pinsker, then
  Cauchy–Schwarz). Decaying depth frequencies tighten it relative to training every depth equally, because
  shallow depths dominate acceptance.
- **Setup (Sec 6, App. A.1):**
  - Model: 10B-A1B latent MoE (Nemotron 3-style), 24 layers, hidden 1,024, 16 heads, multi-head latent attention
    (MLA), gated attention, QK-norm, RoPE. 256 routed experts (8 active) plus 1 shared. Vocabulary 201,216.
  - Data: 600B tokens, sequence 4,096, global batch 1,920, bf16.
  - Optimizer: Muon, with AdamW for embeddings, biases, output head and router. LR 6e-4, warmup 1,000, weight
    decay 0.1, clip 1.0. MTP loss weight 0.3.
  - Systems: 192 H800s, 8-way expert parallelism, ZeRO-1, full recomputation; about 3 days per run.
- **Arms:** ECHO-1 vs MTP-1 (equal cost); ECHO-1.5(3) vs MTP-3 (half the MTP cost); ECHO-3; ECHO-2(7)
  (E[D] = 2 over depths 1–7).

## 4. Key reported results

- **14-benchmark average, step 78k (Table 1):** MTP-1 35.22, MTP-3 35.38, ECHO-1 35.73, **ECHO-1.5(3) 36.34**,
  ECHO-3 36.36, ECHO-2(7) 35.57.
- **By category (Sec 3.1–3.2):**
  - ECHO-1 vs MTP-1: math +0.79, reasoning +0.57, code +1.35.
  - ECHO-3: reasoning +2.63, code +2.23.
  - ECHO-1.5(3): +1.00 / +1.31 / +2.37 over MTP-1, and +0.55 / +2.31 / +1.67 over MTP-3.
  - Going from depth 1 to 3 adds 0.63 points for ECHO but only 0.16 for DeepSeek MTP.
- **Next-token loss does not move (App. E, Fig. 5–6):** ECHO-1 and MTP-1 backbone losses differ by 0.004, with
  ECHO slightly *higher*. All five configurations are within 0.006, and MTP-3 has the lowest (1.676 vs ECHO-3 1.682).
  The authors call the gain a regularization of the representation, visible downstream rather than in loss.
- **Robustness (Sec 5, App. D, Table 2):** ECHO-1 leads MTP-1 by +0.52 in the base run and +0.78 in a repeat run,
  and by +0.50 at twice the token budget (156k steps). Evaluation noise is about 0.13 points on a 14-task gap. The
  reasoning and code lead exceeds run-to-run spread; the QA and multiple-choice difference does not (Fig. 4c).
- **After supervised fine-tuning (SFT) (Table 3):** MTP-Instruct 36.27, ECHO-1.5(3) 37.88 (+1.61), ECHO-2(7)
  38.37 (+2.10).
- **Speculative decoding (Fig. 1b, 3b, 8):**
  - Setup: SGLang on 8×H800 with FlashAttention-3, temperature 1, 7 drafts plus 1 bonus token.
  - Depth: accepted length keeps rising beyond the trained depth, while MTP-3 saturates.
  - Against post-hoc drafters: ECHO has the longest accepted length on high-entropy open-ended tasks vs EAGLE-3,
    DFlash and DSpark.
  - Values exist only in figures; we did not transcribe them.
- **Drafter training avoided (App. A.1):** post-hoc drafters needed 19–55 h on 24 H800s (EAGLE-3 22 h, DFlash
  19 h, DSpark 55 h).

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **Closest to the MoE end of the roadmap.** The test bed is a 10B-total / 1B-active MoE, similar to a ~27B MoE
  with small active compute per token. ECHO's only new parameters are the fusion layer, whereas DeepSeek-style
  MTP adds a whole module per depth. Fewer extra parameters means fewer extra bytes to synchronize under
  DiLoCo/SparseLoCo (2603.08163, 2604.21428).
- **Consumer-GPU memory.** DDS halves *average* MTP compute but not the *peak*. A step that draws D = 3 needs three
  extra final-layer passes plus three output-head projections over a 201k vocabulary. On fixed 8–24 GB cards, the
  D = K case sets the memory budget. Our inference: cap K by device memory, or let small devices use a smaller K.
  Mixing K across peers is untested.
- **More learning per token?** Not on next-token loss, which is identical to MTP. Downstream reasoning and code
  gain about 0.5–1.3 points at the same tokens and FLOPs. There is **no NTP-only baseline**, so the per-FLOP gain
  over plain next-token training is unknown.
- **Verification-friendly design.** The random depth is a pure function of (seed, step), so a replay auditor can
  recompute exactly which depths a peer had to train. Crowd's audits already require seeded,
  CPU-drawn randomness (DEEPDIVE.md); a crowd variant should put (seed, step, D) in the work spec.
- **Serving and RL rollouts on volunteer GPUs.** The pretrained model drafts for itself with no drafter training.
  Speculative decoding is lossless, so drafter quality affects speed, not correctness.
- **Composition.**
  - Soft-MTP (2610.38331) needs the MTP and NTP predictions to share a linear output head. ECHO shares both the
    head and the final layer, so the two should stack. Untested.
  - With SOLO (2609.35440) or other pipeline splits, all recursive passes run on the last stage, which becomes a
    hotspot.

## 6. Caveats

- No pure next-token baseline; every comparison is against DeepSeek MTP.
- Gains are small: 0.5–1.1 points on the 14-task average. There was one repeat run, and SFT used a single seed
  (Limitations).
- Five of the 14 benchmark rows come from an internal evaluation platform whose extraction and judging rules are
  unpublished, so they cannot be reproduced from outside (App. C). Four rows are graded by LLM judges.
- The pretraining corpus and SFT data are proprietary (App. I).
- Only one model shape (10B-A1B MoE, 4k context). No dense or small (≤1B) models.
- No peak-memory or wall-clock numbers for the MTP overhead.
- Nothing on consumer GPUs, low bandwidth or heterogeneous peers.
