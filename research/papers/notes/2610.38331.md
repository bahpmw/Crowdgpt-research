# 2610.38331 — Multi-Token Prediction Done Right (Soft-MTP)

## 1. Citation

- **Title:** Multi-Token Prediction Done Right
- **Author:** Jayden Teoh (MIT). Single author.
- **Date:** arXiv v1, 28 Oct 2026 (cs.LG). Marked "Preprint"; no venue.
- **Code:** https://github.com/JaydenTeoh/Soft-MTP (link from the abstract; we did not check the repo).
- **Compute:** "mostly" 8× RTX PRO 6000 rented on Vast.ai (Acknowledgements). The cost table was measured on one
  H200 (Table 5).

## 2. Summary

Multi-token prediction (MTP) adds K extra heads that predict tokens 2..K+1 ahead. They share the model trunk and
the linear output head W ("LM head") with ordinary next-token prediction (NTP). The paper proves that the MTP
cross-entropy (CE) gradient at the MTP hidden state splits exactly into two parts. The first is a KL (Kullback–Leibler)
self-distillation term that pulls MTP head k toward the model's own NTP distribution at position t+k. The second
is a residual equal to the NTP CE gradient against the one-hot data label at that position. The residual has about 4×
lower signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) but carries about 80% of the MTP gradient that reaches the trunk. A step along it
*raises* NTP loss. **Soft-MTP** removes the residual with a one-line straight-through trick. The MTP heads and
trunk then receive only the KL gradient, and the output head still gets the normal CE gradient. It adds no
parameters and no vocabulary-sized tensors, and it costs the same FLOPs and memory as standard MTP.

## 3. Method

- **Notation (Sec 3–4):** p_k = softmax(W h_k(t)) is MTP head k's prediction of x(t+k+1). q_k = softmax(W h_0(t+k))
  is the position-aligned NTP prediction of the same token.
- **Decomposition (Eq. 2):** g_hard = W^T(p_k − onehot) = W^T(p_k − q_k) [g_KL] + W^T(q_k − onehot) [g_res].
- **Recovery trick (Eq. 4–7):** add `sg(h_k) − h_k` to the NTP hidden state at t+k. The forward value is
  unchanged (the term is zero). In the backward pass, the NTP loss pushes −g_res into the MTP branch, so the branch
  receives g_hard − g_res = g_KL. Algorithm 1 (App. A):
  `h0[:, k:] = h0[:, k:] + s_k * (hk.detach() - hk)` with `s_k = λ·T / (K·(T−k))` to match loss averaging.
- **Gradient routing (Table 1):** standard MTP uses CE at the output head and CE at the heads and trunk.
  KL-MTP (explicit distillation) uses KL at both. **Soft-MTP uses CE at the output head and KL at the heads and
  trunk.** Keeping CE at W avoids a shortcut where KL is lowered by collapsing the readout (Fig. 6).
- **Bias–variance view (Sec 5.3):** dropping g_res removes one-hot sampling noise, which no scale removes. The
  cost is a bias toward the model's own NTP distribution, and that bias shrinks as the model improves. The
  author argues the trade improves with scale.
- **Setup (Sec 6, App. D Table 7):**
  - Model: LLaMA-style, 36 layers, width 512/768/1152/1536, giving NTP paths of 167M/332M/689M/1.2B.
  - MTP heads: DeepSeek-style sequential heads, K=3, λ_MTP=1.0. Each one-layer head is about 3.2% of the trunk's
    non-embedding parameters; the three heads total 99.1M at 1.2B.
  - Data: FineWeb-Edu, 60k steps × 0.5M tokens = 31.5B tokens (about 1.3× Chinchilla at 1.2B), sequence length 2048.
  - Optimizer: Muon (momentum 0.95, Nesterov, 5 Newton–Schulz steps) with AdamW for embeddings, head and norms.
  - Other: GPT-2 vocabulary (50,304), untied embeddings, cosine LR, warmup 2,000, weight decay 0.1, clip 1.0.
- **Parallelism (Sec 7):** h_0(t+k) and h_k(t) must be on the same device. Data, ZeRO and expert parallelism are
  untouched. Pipeline parallelism needs the MTP heads on the output-head stage. Sequence or context parallelism
  adds an exchange of at most K hidden-state gradients per shard boundary.

## 4. Key reported results

- **Gradient diagnostics (Fig. 3–4):** the residual's SNR is about 4× below the KL term's, widening from 4.1× at
  167M to 4.6× at 1.2B. The residual is about 80% of the MTP gradient into the trunk at every scale. A unit step
  along the KL part lowers NTP loss; one along the residual raises it.
- **Zero-shot (Table 3; FineWeb-Edu perplexity / average accuracy):**

| Size | NTP | MTP | Soft-MTP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 167M | 15.230 / 47.42 | 15.983 / 46.84 | 15.952 / 46.91 |
| 332M | 13.073 / 50.79 | 13.602 / 50.38 | 13.551 / 50.46 |
| 689M | 11.442 / 54.79 | 11.834 / 54.47 | 11.724 / 55.01 |
| 1.2B | 10.572 / 56.75 | 10.891 / 56.80 | 10.741 / 57.31 |

  **Plain NTP has the best FineWeb-Edu perplexity at every size.** Soft-MTP beats NTP on average accuracy only at
  689M and 1.2B. At 1.2B it also has the best WikiText (17.489 vs 17.503) and LAMBADA (14.073 vs 14.159) perplexity.
- **Self-speculative decoding (Table 2):** at 1.2B on FineWeb-Edu, speedup is 1.76× vs 1.74× for MTP, and
  acceptance length is 1.70 vs 1.67 tokens per draft beyond the always-accepted one. Averaged over 5 domains:
  +1.8–2.2% acceptance length and +1.4–1.5% speedup at each scale. Speedup hardware is not stated (unverified).
- **After NTP-only fine-tuning at 1.2B (Table 4; NTP / MTP / Soft-MTP):**
  - HumanEval pass@64: 39.02 / 40.85 / 43.90.
  - GSM8K: 47.69 / 48.60 / 49.66.
  - MATH-500: 24.80 / 23.20 / 25.00.
  - Soft-MTP never falls below NTP.
- **Cost at 1.2B, K=3, one H200 (Table 5; NTP / MTP / Soft-MTP):**
  - Matmul FLOPs per token: 8.16 / 10.28 / 10.28 GFLOP, so +26% for any MTP.
  - Throughput: 62,851 / 49,783 / 49,748 tok/s, about −21%.
  - Peak memory with fused CE at micro-batch 19: 95.5 / 107.0 / 107.0 GB.
  - Explicit KL-MTP: 128.1 GB and −5.6% throughput versus MTP.
- **Readout effective rank (Fig. 6):** NTP 496, MTP 131, KL-MTP 321, Soft-MTP 437.
- **Sweeps (Fig. 5, 332M, 7.3B tokens):** Soft-MTP has lower NTP and MTP loss than MTP for every
  λ ∈ {0.25…1.0} and K ∈ {3…8}. Meta-style parallel heads move the same way (App. C).

## 5. Relevance to Crowd

- **More learning per token, honestly scored.** At 167M–1.2B and 31.5B tokens, no MTP variant beats NTP on
  in-distribution perplexity, and every MTP variant costs +26% FLOPs per token (Tables 3, 5). The gains are
  downstream: zero-shot accuracy from 689M up, code and math after fine-tuning, and a built-in drafter. For the
  1B stage, MTP is not a per-FLOP win on loss. Soft-MTP is the variant to use if we adopt MTP at all; switching
  to it from standard MTP costs nothing.
- **Why it can still fit a crowd.** A DiLoCo/SparseLoCo-style network (2603.08163, 2604.21428) is limited by
  bytes, not local FLOPs. Extra per-token compute adds no traffic, except that the head parameters must also be
  synchronized: 99.1M on a 1.17B NTP path, +8.4% per pseudo-gradient. Syncing heads less often is an untested idea.
  The paper cites NanoGPT Slowrun (qlabs-eng/slowrun) for MTP improving sample efficiency when data is fixed.
  This matters if a million-GPU crowd becomes data-limited (we did not verify the Slowrun claim).
- **Consumer-GPU memory.** MTP adds about 11.5 GB at micro-batch 19 at 1.2B, even with fused CE (Table 5). On
  8–24 GB cards this needs fused CE kernels and smaller micro-batches. Explicit KL distillation (+21 GB) is not
  practical; Soft-MTP adds nothing over MTP.
- **Inference and RL on volunteer GPUs.** The MTP heads are a free self-drafter (about 1.7× at 1.2B). Speculative
  decoding verifies every draft, so a poisoned or poorly synced head can only cost speed, never change outputs.
- **Composition.**
  - Soft-MTP changes only the objective, so it is orthogonal to the outer optimizer, compression and Muon.
  - With SOLO (2609.35440): SOLO's local heads read a shared copy of the output head. The KL gradient-recovery
    trick applies "whenever a teacher and a student share a linear output head" (Sec 7), so it might distill local
    heads toward the final head without vocabulary-sized logits. Untested idea.
  - Pipeline splits put all MTP compute on the last stage, which risks load imbalance.
- **Trust/audit.** No randomness and no new kernels, so replay reproducibility is unchanged versus MTP. A
  validator such as Gauntlet's LossScore (2603.08163) could add a cheap check: the KL between the MTP heads and
  the NTP head after a peer's update. Untested idea.

## 6. Caveats

- Single-author preprint. The tables report one run per configuration (no seeds or error bars) and there is no
  independent replication.
- Maximum 1.2B parameters and 31.5B tokens. "Better with scale" is an extrapolation from 4 points.
- One dataset (FineWeb-Edu), 2048 context, dense models only. No MoE and no long context.
- Untested: low-communication training, heterogeneous fleets, stale or partial synchronization of MTP heads, and
  consumer GPUs below workstation class.
