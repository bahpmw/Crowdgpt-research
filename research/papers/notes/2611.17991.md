# 2611.17991 — LACCO: Local Accumulation with Computation–Communication Overlap (LACCO)

## 1. Citation
- **Title:** LACCO: Local Accumulation with Computation–Communication Overlap
- **Authors:** Daniele Kopyshevskiy (Sorbonne Université, ISIR), Benjamin Thérien (Mila, Université de
  Montréal), Nicolas Pinto (Cygni Labs, Keytsugi), Edouard Oyallon (Sorbonne Université, CNRS)
- **Date:** arXiv v1, 13 Nov 2026 (cs.AI), preprint, 34 pages. **Venue:** none stated.
- **Code:** https://github.com/kopyshevskiy/lacco (stated in Sec. 1; repository not checked).
- **Builds on:** ACCO (Nabli et al. 2025), which uses two buffers with one step per round (H = 1). Compared
  against Eager and Delayed DiLoCo (Kale et al. 2025).

## 2. Summary
Even DiLoCo (Distributed Low-Communication training) stalls at every outer step. The replicas wait for the
all-reduce of their pseudo-gradients (parameter deltas) and for the outer update. There are two existing ways to
hide that wait:
- Apply the previous round's average (Delayed DiLoCo). The update is stale.
- Mix in each replica's own fresh pseudo-gradient (Eager DiLoCo). Replicas then drift apart, and the problem
  grows with the number of replicas K.

LACCO keeps two parameter copies (θ and θ̃) and alternates two stages of H inner steps each. While one stage
computes, the previous stage's all-reduce runs in the background. Each outer update uses only averages that have
already finished and that were computed from the current pair of copies. Every replica therefore applies the same
update, and nothing is stale. Results:
- Up to 0.5B parameters and 16 replicas, it beats Eager and Delayed DiLoCo and stays within 1% of DiLoCo across
  K = 2–16 at H = 30 (Table 6).
- At H = 30 it hides communication fully: 1.87× faster end-to-end than DiLoCo at 0.5 Gbit/s (0.5B model).

## 3. Method
Sec. 3.3; Algorithm 1 in App. A. Per outer iteration t:
1. **Lookahead stage.** From θ_t, run H inner AdamW steps (InnerOpt_M), giving `g_t^i = θ_t − θ_{t,H}^i`.
   Launch an async all-reduce of g_t. Wait for g̃_t (launched in the previous stage), then set
   `θ̃_{t+1} = OuterOpt_L(θ_t, g̃_t)`.
2. **Main stage.** From θ̃_{t+1}, run H inner steps (InnerOpt_L), giving g̃_{t+1}^i. Launch its async
   all-reduce. Wait for g_t, then set `θ_{t+1} = OuterOpt_M(θ_t, ½(g_t + g̃_t))`.

Key properties:
- One stage's communication hides under the next stage's H steps. The number of communications per token equals
  DiLoCo with the same H.
- State: two parameter sets, two inner AdamW states and two outer Nesterov states. The inactive AdamW state can
  be offloaded to CPU and prefetched (Sec. 3.3).
- The two inner AdamW momenta are averaged after each iteration. This was the best of three policies: 2.988 vs
  3.008 (shared state) and 3.029 (rewind) (App. E.2, Table 10).
- **Overlap condition (App. D):** communication is fully hidden if `T_inner ≥ T_comm + T_outer`. With offload,
  LACCO moves 5 fp32 buffers per outer step against 2 for the others. In their H100 timing model it therefore
  needs H ≥ 19, against 9 for Delayed DiLoCo (Table 9).
- **Theory:** a smooth non-convex rate for SGD inner/outer optimizers (Thm 1, Eq. 14), with η = 30 and
  βL ≤ 1/30.
- **Setup (Sec. 4, App. B):**
  - Models: Llama-3-style 134M/325M/500M on FineWeb, 20 tokens per parameter, sequence length 2048.
  - Global batch 64 sequences (134M, 325M) or 128 (500M); default K = 4 replicas, H = 30.
  - Optimizers: AdamW (0.9, 0.95). β2 is not scaled with K. LACCO outer: learning rate 0.7, momentum 0.9
    (Table 8).
  - Software: TorchTitan + TorchFT, NCCL.

## 4. Key reported results
- **134M, K = 4, H = 30 (Table 2), evaluation loss:** distributed data parallel (DDP) 2.936, DiLoCo 2.961,
  LACCO 2.988, Eager DiLoCo 3.020, Delayed DiLoCo 3.117. Table 5 lists LACCO at 3.001 for H = 30, apparently a
  different run (unexplained).
- **Model size (Table 4), evaluation loss:**
  - 325M: DDP 2.673, DiLoCo 2.692, LACCO 2.693, Eager 2.715, Delayed 2.795.
  - 500M: DDP 2.558, DiLoCo 2.557, LACCO 2.561, Eager 2.576, Delayed 2.675.
- **H sweep (Table 5, 134M, K = 4):**
  - LACCO roughly matches DiLoCo at H = 10–15 (2.960/2.959 vs 2.959/2.960).
  - The gap widens at large H: at H = 75, 3.103 vs 3.016.
  - Eager DiLoCo collapses at H = 5 (4.370).
- **Replicas (Table 6, 134M, H = 30, fixed global batch), K = 2/4/8/16:**
  - DiLoCo 2.941/2.961/3.029/3.143; LACCO 2.953/2.988/3.054/3.154.
  - Eager 2.946/3.020/3.183/3.445; Delayed 3.027/3.117/3.209/3.353.
- **Measured wall-clock (Table 3; 0.5B, K = 4, H = 30, A100 80 GB, throttled inter-node links), time per round:**
  - 0.5 Gbit/s: DiLoCo 101.17 s vs LACCO 54.05 s with CPU offload (1.87×).
  - 1 Gbit/s: 1.44×. 5 Gbit/s: 1.07×.
- **Memory and throughput (Table 11):**
  - Peak GPU memory: DiLoCo 37.9 GiB; LACCO with offload 40.3 GiB (+2.4 GiB); LACCO fully on GPU 56.1 GiB.
  - Throughput at 0.5 Gbit/s: 77.7k tokens/s for DiLoCo vs 145.5k for LACCO.
- **Drift between the copies (App. E.1, Fig. 4):** the gap between θ and θ̃ peaks early (around 0.35B tokens, larger
  for larger H) and then shrinks to about zero by the end.

## 5. Relevance to Crowd
- **What it hides.** In any round-based design (Covenant-style rounds, Decoupled DiLoCo fragments), uploading
  and downloading compressed pseudo-gradients sits on the critical path. On home uplinks that can be a large share
  of a round. LACCO hides one full round of communication behind the next H local steps, with neither staleness
  (Delayed) nor replica drift (Eager).
- **Warning for "use my own update while I wait" tricks at scale.** Eager DiLoCo degrades sharply with K (3.445
  vs 3.154 for LACCO at K = 16, Table 6). Tricks of this kind will likely get worse at large peer counts.
- **Memory on volunteer machines (our arithmetic from App. D's 3 extra fp32 buffers).** The second copy plus the
  inactive AdamW state can live in host RAM: about 12 GB extra host RAM per volunteer for a 1B model. The GPU-side
  overhead was only +2.4 GiB at 0.5B (Table 11). Consumer PCIe bandwidth against step time is unverified.
- **Composes with:**
  - SparseLoCo compression, which is orthogonal (Sec. 2 says LACCO needs no change to compression methods).
  - The β2 rule of 2610.19064, not used here.
  - Decoupled DiLoCo's asynchronous quorum, which the authors call possible but leave untested.
  - Gossip (GASLoC/SPICE), where the two-buffer trick could hide a gossip exchange (untested).
- **Trust.** All replicas apply the same averaged update, so "did this peer apply the right global update?" stays
  as easy to check as in DiLoCo. However, pseudo-gradients alternate between two base models (θ and θ̃). A
  validator (for example Covenant's Gauntlet, which scores the loss change from applying a submission) must know
  which base each submission belongs to and score against it.
- **Relation to round 1.** Decoupled DiLoCo (2604.21428) already overlaps τ = 2 steps by overwriting fragments;
  LACCO overlaps a whole H-step stage with no staleness. It does not address churn, stragglers or Byzantine
  peers. It complements Decoupled DiLoCo (asynchrony) and Soft Throttling (2609.39357, robustness); it does not
  replace them.

## 6. Caveats
- **Small and synchronous.**
  - At most 0.5B parameters, K ≤ 16, and a global batch of only 64–128 sequences.
  - The authors' "primary target setting is a tightly interconnected GPU cluster" (Sec. 4.1); their timing
    model assumes 10 GB/s.
  - The real measurement covers only 4 A100s at 0.5–5 Gbit/s.
  - Every replica must join every all-reduce; stragglers and failures are explicitly out of scope (Sec. 2).
- **Large H.** The gap to DiLoCo grows with H (+2.9% at H = 75, our arithmetic from Table 5), and Crowd wants
  large H on slow links.
- **Window size.** Communication must fit inside one H-step stage. On 20 Mbit/s uplinks with a 1B model, even
  compressed rounds may not fit (unverified).
- **β2 fixed at 0.95 while K grows.** All methods may look worse at K = 16 than they need to (see 2610.19064).
- **Reporting.** LACCO's H = 30 number is inconsistent (2.988 in Table 2 vs 3.001 in Table 5). No seeds or error
  bars are reported (we did not find them). Theory covers SGD only, with unusual constants (η = 30).
